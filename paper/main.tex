\begin{abstract}
\noindent
1.~Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (AMF) are identified from environmental DNA with three naming systems based on
different rRNA gene regions: MaarjAM virtual taxa (VTs; small-subunit, SSU), UNITE species hypotheses (SHs; internal
transcribed spacer, ITS) and species clades on large-subunit (LSU) reference trees. The names rarely agree, and rRNA
gene copies differ within a genome, so one fungus can appear as several taxa and a divergent copy as a new one. We
asked how the systems relate, whether combining them identifies species reliably, and how often species missing
from the reference are mistaken for known ones.

\noindent
2.~We named \NCopiesRetained{} long-read sequences spanning the SSU, ITS and LSU, from \NCultures{} cultures of
\NSpecies{} species, in all three systems. We removed each culture and each species from the reference in turn, also
removed species from the LSU reference, and tested \LgTestCultures{} cultures from other collections and \EkTestN{}
EUKARYOME sequences against predictions fixed in advance.

\noindent
3.~SHs were mostly nested within LSU species, and these within VTs: VTs separated at most \VTResolvedZero{} of
\NSpecies{} species, whereas single cultures spanned up to \SHSplitMax{} SHs. In \spn{Rhizoglomus}, sequences with a
shortened 5.8S gene shared one deletion across five species; i.e., they are genomic paralogs rather than artifacts.
On trees, species formed clades but conspecific cultures did not. With one culture removed, the combined names kept
the correct species for \KnownKeptITS{} of \KnownN{} sequences. Species removed from both the culture and LSU
references were still identified as known for \MfourFKvTwoPct{} of their sequences (95\% interval over species
\CiMfourVTwoSp{}), against \MfourControlTwoPct{} with the LSU reference intact. These errors fell within species
complexes that the markers cannot separate; predictions from the reference flagged every species at risk, but not
every misidentified sequence. In cultures from other collections, the only misidentifications (\LgFK{} of
\LgNovelN{} sequences) were those predicted. Of \EkNovelN{} EUKARYOME sequences of absent taxa, \EkFK{}
(\EkFKPct{}) were identified as known, all of them close relatives of reference species, and \EkFxOutside{} of these
were not predicted, mostly because they matched reference cultures identified only to genus.

\noindent
4.~Novelty can be inferred from rRNA genes only where the markers separate species, and error rates hold only for the
reference databases used. For diversity analyses, divergent copies should be recognized before taxa are counted, and
inseparable species should be reported as complexes.

\vspace{0.6em}
\noindent
\textbf{Keywords.} arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi; Glomeromycota; rRNA gene intragenomic variation; MaarjAM virtual
taxa; UNITE species hypotheses; novelty detection; phylogenetic placement; long-read metabarcoding.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ============================================================================
Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (AMF; Glomeromycota) live in symbiosis with the roots of about 72\% of vascular plant
species \cite{brundrett2018evolutionary}. Because AMF in roots and soil can rarely be identified by their
morphology, their communities are studied mostly by metabarcoding: a marker region of the rRNA genes is amplified
from environmental DNA, sequenced, and compared with reference databases
\cite{opik2010maarjam,davison2015global,vetrovsky2023globalamfungi}.

Surveys of AMF name what they find in three incompatible ways, so results from different markers cannot be pooled.
There are three well-established methods of identification, all based on the rRNA locus, which holds the genes for
the small-subunit (SSU, 18S), 5.8S and large-subunit (LSU, 28S) rRNAs, separated by two internal transcribed
spacers, ITS1 and ITS2 (together with the 5.8S gene, the ITS region; Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}a). Many SSU-based
studies refer to MaarjAM virtual taxa (VTs), i.e., pre-established groups of SSU sequences defined by phylogeny and
at least 97\% sequence similarity \cite{opik2010maarjam,opik2014virtual}. ITS-based studies commonly use UNITE
species hypotheses (SHs), i.e., clusters of ITS sequences at flexible distance thresholds
\cite{koljalg2013unified,nilsson2019unite}. LSU-based studies usually place reads on curated reference trees and
name them after the species clade in which they fall
\cite{kruger2012phylogenetic,delavaux2021lsu,delavaux2022lsu,delavaux2024lsu}. The ITS and LSU approaches usually
give Latin species names, whereas these are mostly lacking for VTs. Apart from the species names shared by the ITS
and LSU approaches, there is no conversion from one system to another. Therefore, global occurrence databases such
as GlobalAMFungi keep these datasets apart \cite{vetrovsky2023globalamfungi}. The codes of the roughly
species-level units in the three databases have evolved independently, and many reference sequences are
unidentified environmental clones, some of which have been recognized as chimeric \cite{tedersoo2024eukaryome}.

AMF genomes make the comparison harder, because their rRNA gene copies are not homogenized. Copies within one
individual differ \cite{pawlowska2004organization}, and a single \spn{Rhizoglomus irregulare} genome carries only
ten or eleven complete rRNA gene units, which differ in sequence and are not arranged in tandem
\cite{maeda2018rdna}. Furthermore, the rRNA gene copies within a culture can differ by several percent in the ITS
region \cite{vankuren2013rdna,stefani2025rdna}, and variation in the LSU and SSU genes can also be high (here,
within cultures, up to several percent in the LSU but rarely more than 1\% in the SSU; Section~\ref{sec:nest} and
Methods S3). One genome can therefore look
like several taxa, and a divergent copy can look like an unknown lineage or an undescribed species.

Short-read metabarcoding allows only one short marker to be analysed at a time. By contrast, long reads that span
the SSU, ITS and LSU link all three markers in a single sequence. Amplicons of about 2.5~kb were introduced to bring
the three marker regions into one dataset \cite{kolarikova2021pacbio}, and a large collection of such sequences
from identified cultures now exists \cite{stefani2025rdna}. In this collection, every sequence can be named in all
three systems at once, and the species name of each culture serves as the known answer for its own sequences. We
used it to ask four questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item How do VTs, SHs and LSU-based species clades correspond to each other and to cultured species?
\item To what extent do divergent rRNA gene copies interfere with species recognition and naming?
\item Does combining the three naming systems identify species more reliably than any one of them?
\item When a species is missing from the reference, how often is it misidentified as a known species, and how does
this depend on what the reference databases contain?
\end{enumerate}

Throughout, a species is \emph{new} or \emph{unknown} when it is absent from the reference against which a
sequence is identified. Our tests remove species from the culture reference and, in one test, from the LSU reference
as well. None removes a species from every database, so none measures the error for a species that has never been
sequenced, and each error rate is reported together with the reference it was measured against. We evaluate the
identification of individual sequences, from cultures and from a curated database; we do not evaluate community
profiles or richness estimates.

\section{Materials and methods}
% ============================================================================
\subsection{Culture sequences and marker regions}\label{sec:data}
We used the \NCopiesIn{} rRNA gene sequences from \StefaniCultures{} cultures of \StefaniSpecies{} species
deposited by Stefani et al.\ \cite{stefani2025rdna} (GenBank PX207096--PX207274 and PX214440--PX215164). Each
sequence is a PacBio read of about 2.8~kb that spans most of the SSU gene, the whole ITS region and the first part of
the LSU gene (Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}a). We call them sequences rather than copies: each comes from one rRNA gene
copy, but two identical sequences may come from the same copy, and a sequence may carry PCR or sequencing errors.
Stefani et al.\ also sequenced three protein-coding genes, which we used only to examine species that the rRNA genes
cannot separate (Section~\ref{sec:stefani}). Three culture labels hold two lineages each (mixed cultures), which we
treated as separate cultures, yielding \NCultures{} cultures in total. We used the taxon names as deposited, except
that species deposited in \spn{Rhizophagus} are given here in \spn{Rhizoglomus}
\cite{sieverding2015rhizoglomus}, the name we use for this genus (e.g., \spn{Rhizoglomus irregulare} for
\spn{Rhizophagus irregularis}); the databases use either name, and we treated the two as synonyms throughout.

In each sequence, ITSxRust \cite{obrien2026itsxrust} located the SSU, ITS1, 5.8S, ITS2 and LSU, and we extracted, at
the original primer sites, the fragment on which each database was built: NS31--AM1 for MaarjAM
\cite{simon1992specific,helgason1998ploughing,opik2010maarjam}, the full ITS region (ITS1, 5.8S and ITS2) for UNITE,
and LR0R--FLR2 for the LSU reference \cite{bunyard1994morchella,trouvelot1999visualization,delavaux2021lsu}. Four
additional marker fragments used in AMF studies were extracted in the same way (Table~\ref{tab:stage1}; Methods
S1). For phylogenetic trees and rRNA structure, we used the whole length of each sequence, split into three regions:
the \emph{SSU region} (all SSU sequence in the read), the full ITS region, and the \emph{LSU region} (all LSU
sequence in the read).

The culture reference, i.e., the curated set of culture sequences against which other sequences are compared, was
built from the deposited sequences and a list of curation edits (Table~\ref{tab:edits}; Methods S2). Each edit was
written together with a check of what the data had to look like, and the build stopped if any check failed. One
sequence was removed as a probable error or pseudogene (Section~\ref{sec:copies}). The final reference (version~4)
holds \NCopiesRetained{} sequences of \NCultures{} cultures and \RefNames{} species: \NSpecies{} named species and
\RefGenusOnly{} species identified to genus only.

Within a culture, most sequences share one main rRNA gene type. We sorted the sequences of each culture into three
classes by their 5.8S gene, comparing each with the culture's medoid, i.e., the sequence closest to all others of
its culture:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{main-type} sequences, whose 5.8S differs by at most 3\% from that of the medoid;
\item \emph{short-5.8S} sequences, whose 5.8S differs by more than 3\% and is at least 3~bp shorter than that of
the culture's main type;
\item \emph{same-length} sequences, whose 5.8S differs by more than 3\% but is not shorter.
\end{enumerate}
Short-5.8S and same-length sequences together are called \emph{divergent} sequences.

The distance between two sequences was the number of substitutions, insertions and deletions needed to align the
shorter sequence within the longer one (the edit, or Levenshtein, distance, with free end gaps in the longer
sequence), expressed as a percentage of the length of the shorter sequence (computed with edlib
\cite{sosic2017edlib}).

\subsection{Naming each sequence in the three systems}\label{sec:naming}
Each sequence was searched with BLAST+ \cite{camacho2009blast} against the database of each system, with
marker-specific identity and coverage thresholds (Table~\ref{tab:arms}): its SSU region against MaarjAM (the gDAT
snapshot of July 2020 \cite{opik2010maarjam}), whose references span the NS31--AM1 fragment; its full ITS region
against UNITE~10.0 (the full UNITE+INSD set of 19 February 2025 \cite{nilsson2019unite,unite2025release}); and its
LR0R--FLR2 fragment against the LSU reference of Delavaux et al.\ (V18 of May 2025
\cite{delavaux2021lsu,delavaux2022lsu,delavaux2024lsu}). Hits to any sequence of the culture reference were
disregarded, so that no sequence could take its name from itself or from its own culture.

The best BLAST hit can change with one or two differences in a sequence. Each sequence therefore kept not only the
name of its best hit but every name whose hit was almost as good, within a margin of identity of the best hit; we
call these its \emph{near-best names}. For each system, the margin was the smallest at which the main-type sequences
of at least 99\% of cultures share at least one name. In addition, where the main-type sequences of one culture were
split between two names, and the sequences on either side of the split differed by less than 1\% in the SSU, the
two names were merged (Table~\ref{tab:sets}; Methods S3).

For the LSU, sequences were also placed on the V18 reference tree with EPA-ng \cite{barbera2019epang}. A placement
within a clade that holds records of a single V18 species was read as that species (the \emph{LSU species clade};
Methods S4).

\subsection{Combining the three names}\label{sec:combining}
Each name in each system (a VT, an SH or an LSU species clade) was linked to the species and genera of the
reference cultures whose sequences have the same name (Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}b). A name was \emph{matched} when
at least one reference culture has it and \emph{unmatched} when none does; a sequence with no BLAST hit passing the
thresholds of a system had no name in that system. The \emph{consensus assignment} kept only what the matched names
agree on: the genera common to all matched names, and, among the matched names linked to named species, the species
common to them. For example, a VT shared by cultures of \spn{Glomus chinense} and \spn{Glomus rugosae}, together with
an LSU species clade held only by \spn{Glomus rugosae}, gives \spn{Glomus rugosae}. The outcome was a species; a
species group (several species that the names cannot separate); a genus only, when the matched names agree on a
genus but not on a species; \emph{discordant}, when the matched names point to different genera; or no assignment,
when no name was matched.

\subsection{Deciding when a sequence is new}\label{sec:novelty}
A sequence was considered new when its species, or its lineage, is not represented in the culture reference. Before
a sequence was considered new, three simpler explanations were tested in a fixed order (Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}b;
Methods S5):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{SSU discordance (possible artifact).} Compared with the nearest main-type sequence of another culture,
the SSU differs by at least 1\% and by at least half as much as the full ITS. Between cultures the SSU changes far
more slowly than the ITS (Section~\ref{sec:trees}), whereas errors spread along a read, and copies freed from
selection, change both at similar rates. The flag marks an SSU unlike that of any cultured relative; it is not a
chimera screen (Section~\ref{sec:trees}).
\item \textbf{Divergent sequence.} The sequence's 5.8S is at least 3~bp shorter than, and at least 3\% different
from, the main-type 5.8S of the nearest culture; or its best VT or SH is reached, in at least two other cultures,
only by short-5.8S sequences (a \emph{paralog name}). Such sequences are named by their VT alone, because their SSU
is that of their genome. A same-length 5.8S that differs by 3\% or more is only flagged.
\item \textbf{Near a VT threshold.} The sequence has no VT name, but its best VT hit lies within one percentage
point of the 97\% threshold.
\item \textbf{New.} An assignment to a species was changed to \emph{new species in a known genus} when the VT or the
LSU name is unmatched, when the matched names share a genus but no species, or when the full ITS lies farther from
every reference culture than the within-species limit of the ITS (Section~\ref{sec:complexes}; the \emph{ITS
distance rule}). A sequence none of whose names is matched was classified as a \emph{new lineage}, which has no
genus. An unmatched SH does not change an assignment, because the rRNA gene copies of a single genome often fall
into several SHs (Section~\ref{sec:nest}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Species complexes}\label{sec:complexes}
Some named species cannot be distinguished by any of the three markers, and we identified these from the reference
alone. For each marker (full ITS, LR0R--FLR2 and SSU region), we measured the distance from each main-type sequence
to the nearest main-type sequence of another culture of the same species, and took the 99th percentile of these
distances as the \emph{within-species limit}: \ITSSpread{} in the ITS, \LSUSpread{} in the LSU and \SSUSpread{} in
the SSU. Two named species were considered \emph{inseparable} when, on every marker, their closest sequences were no
farther apart than this limit, so that a sequence of one falls within the variation of the other. Inseparable pairs
that share a species were joined into a \emph{species complex}, which is reported as one unit, a species group,
wherever a species is assigned. The rule uses no test result and adds no threshold beyond the percentile already
used by the ITS distance rule. Because that percentile was chosen after the distances in these cultures had been
seen, the ITS distance rule and the complexes were also recomputed together at the 95th and 97.5th percentiles
(Methods S6).

The rule can be used in two ways. Applied to the species of the reference, it defines the complexes, a
retrospective statement about these cultures. Applied to a new sequence, it says whether that sequence lies
\emph{inside} a reference species, i.e., within the within-species limit of that species on all three markers. If
the complexes mark the limit of what the markers can resolve, only such a sequence can be misidentified as that
species. This use needs no test result, and we used it to fix the predictions of the two external tests before they
were run (Sections~\ref{sec:lgdb-methods} and~\ref{sec:euk-methods}). After review, we also applied it within each
round of the LSU-removal test (Section~\ref{sec:tests}), with the withheld cultures removed and the limits
recomputed without them (Methods S6).

\subsection{Tests with withheld cultures and species}\label{sec:tests}
We evaluated the assignments with leave-one-out tests: part of the reference was removed (withheld), and the
withheld sequences were identified against the rest. In each test round, everything learned from the cultures (the
merged names, the margins, the links from names to species, the comparisons of the divergent-sequence test, the
paralog names and the limit of the ITS distance rule) was recomputed from the remaining cultures only (Methods S6).

\emph{Withheld cultures.} In 148 rounds, one culture label was withheld, together with both lineages where a label
holds a mixed culture. We asked whether the main-type sequences of species still represented by another culture
were assigned to a known species or species group that includes their own species.

\emph{Withheld species.} In 50 rounds, one named species was withheld with all its cultures (\NSpecies{} rounds),
or one culture not named to species (13 rounds). A \emph{false-known assignment} is an assignment to a known
species or species group for a sequence whose species has no culture left in the reference.

\emph{Withheld from the LSU reference as well.} In the tests above, the three databases stayed intact, so a
withheld species could still have its own VT, SH or LSU species clade. In the \emph{LSU-removal test}, each withheld
species was also removed from the LSU reference: the reference alignment was rebuilt without its records, the model
refitted and all sequences placed again. MaarjAM and UNITE were not edited, so a withheld species kept any VT or SH
it has there; this matters because an unmatched VT changes an assignment to `new' and an unmatched SH does not.
This test is the closest of ours to a species that has never been sequenced, but it does not equal one. The ITS
distance rule was added after two species, \spn{Glomus chinense} and \spn{Glomus rugosae}, had been run in this
test; we report these as \emph{development species}, because they were used to develop the rule. The
\MfourFolds{} other species whose LSU records could be removed (the \emph{test species}) were run after the rule had
been fixed; \spn{\MfourAbsentTarget}, which the LSU reference does not hold by name, is reported apart. In
\GenusFolds{} further rounds, all cultures of a genus and the genus's LSU records were withheld together.

\emph{Uncertainty.} Sequences of one culture, and cultures of one species, are not independent test cases. The main
error rates are therefore given with 95\% intervals from cluster bootstraps that resample species, or cultures,
with replacement (10,000 replicates), together with the numbers of species and cultures that contribute errors
(Table~\ref{tab:intervals}; Methods S6).

These cultures were also used to develop the methods, so every result from them is a development estimate.

\subsection{Cultures from other collections}\label{sec:lgdb-methods}
To test the method on cultures not used to develop it, we used LongGloDB v1 (July 2026) \cite{glodb}, which holds
curated SSU--ITS--LSU sequences of AMF cultures from several collections, given as amplicon sequence variants
(ASVs), i.e., exact sequence variants inferred from long-read amplicons. After removing the ASVs of the Stefani et
al.\ cultures and of cultures sharing their culture codes (Methods S7), the test set holds \LgTestASVs{} ASVs of
\LgTestCultures{} cultures from three other laboratories. Of these, \LgKnownN{} ASVs (\LgKnownCultures{} cultures)
belong to species present in our reference; \LgNovelSpN{} belong to species absent from the reference but in genera
present in it; \LgNovelLinN{} belong to genera absent from the reference; and \LgGenusOnlyN{} are identified to genus
only. The absent species are represented by \LgNovelCultures{} cultures of \LgNovelSpecies{} species.

Before any test ASV was searched, placed or scored, we wrote down the predictions of the complex rule
(Section~\ref{sec:complexes}) for every test ASV, together with a protocol and two amendments that set the
outcomes and analyses. We fixed these files by publishing their checksums, i.e., digital fingerprints that reveal
any later change (Methods S7); this amounts to a pre-registration. Under the rule, a test ASV can be wrongly
identified as a known species only if it lies inside that species on all three markers, so a false-known assignment
outside the predicted set would contradict the result of Section~\ref{sec:errorcomplexes}. The rules were used
unchanged and learned only from the \NCultures{} reference cultures, so no test ASV influenced them. The test ASVs
were processed like the culture sequences and named against the same database releases, first with the LSU
reference intact and then with each of the \LgFolds{} absent species removed from it in its own round, as in the
LSU-removal test.

The test ASVs begin about 60~bp inside the NS31--AM1 fragment, so they rarely cover the 95\% of a MaarjAM reference
that the VT search requires: only \LgVTAnchor{} of the \LgTestASVs{} ASVs had a VT hit that passed. We therefore ran
two analyses, both fixed in advance: the main analysis applied the rules as specified, and a second analysis counted
VT coverage only over the part of each MaarjAM reference that the ASV spans, changing nothing else.

\subsection{Sequences from EUKARYOME}\label{sec:euk-methods}
The species absent from the LongGloDB test were mostly distant from those of the reference. To test close relatives
of reference species, and absent lineages in large numbers, we used the long-read sequences of EUKARYOME v2.1
\cite{tedersoo2024eukaryome}. These are environmental and reference sequences that span the SSU, ITS and LSU and
carry EUKARYOME's classification rather than a culture name. Of its \EkReads{} Glomeromycota sequences whose three
regions each span at least 300~nt, \EkWindows{} were processed like the culture sequences. Their names were matched
to those of the reference with a fixed table, written before any sequence was compared with anything: names in
\spn{Rhizophagus} were read as \spn{Rhizoglomus}, epithets were matched on their stem, and seven names whose relation
to the reference is uncertain were decided one by one, each with its reason recorded. EUKARYOME's placeholder
lineages, which have no named member, count as genera that the reference does not hold. We removed the
\EkIdenticalN{} sequences whose full ITS is identical to a sequence of the reference (\EkIdenticalAssembly{} of them
rRNA gene copies from genome assemblies), and \EkOtherRemovedN{} others that lacked a genus or one of the three
regions. The test set holds \EkTestN{} sequences: \EkKnownN{} of \EkKnownSpecies{} species present in the reference;
\EkNovelSpN{} of \EkNovelSpSpecies{} species absent from it, in genera present in it; \EkNovelLinN{} of lineages
absent from it (\EkNovelLinNamedN{} of \EkNovelLinGenera{} named genera and \EkPlaceholderN{} of
\EkPlaceholderTaxa{} placeholder lineages); and \EkGenusOnlyN{} identified only to a genus present in the reference,
which cannot be scored. None is identical in full ITS to a LongGloDB ASV. EUKARYOME's names are a classification,
not culture names, and are less reliable at species level; they are independent of the method, but EUKARYOME is
curated by one of us (L.T.).

As in the LongGloDB test, the predictions of the complex rule and a protocol fixing the outcomes, conditions and
analyses were fixed before any sequence was searched, placed or scored (Methods S7). With the within-species limits
of Section~\ref{sec:complexes}, only \EkLockedN{} of the \EkNovelN{} sequences of absent taxa lies inside a
reference species (\EkLockedCase{}). Each absent species that V18 holds was removed from the LSU reference in its
own round (\EkRoundsSp{} rounds), and each absent genus that V18 holds with all its records (\EkRoundsGen{} rounds);
the \EkApartN{} sequences of taxa that V18 does not name, \EkPlaceholderN{} of them in placeholder lineages, were
scored with the LSU reference intact. Most sequences start \EkVTStart{} inside the NS31--AM1 fragment, close to the
coverage that the VT search requires, so the VT search was run as specified and, in a second analysis also fixed in
advance, with coverage counted over the part of each MaarjAM reference that a sequence spans. Any genus given to a
sequence of an absent genus counts as wrong, except for \spn{Nanoglomus}: \EkNanoInsideN{} of its sequences lie
inside reference cultures named only as \spn{Glomus} sp.\ on all three markers, so these cultures may be
\spn{Nanoglomus}. After the results were in, we examined the false-known assignments outside the prediction by
measuring their distance on each marker to every reference culture, including those identified only to genus
(\path{analysis/euk_followup.py}); this analysis is exploratory.

\subsection{Phylogenetic trees, artifact check and rRNA structure}\label{sec:checkmethods}
The phylogenetic trees, the artifact check and the structure analysis were specified in a protocol whose checksum
was published before they were run; the structure analysis was added in a dated addendum, likewise fixed before its results were seen (Methods S8).

\emph{Phylogenetic trees.} For each taxonomic order, identical sequences were collapsed and the sequences aligned
with MAFFT L-INS-i \cite{katoh2013mafft}, separately for the SSU region, the full ITS and the LSU region, and for
the three joined end to end (the \emph{concatenated sequence}). Maximum-likelihood trees were inferred with
IQ-TREE~2 \cite{minh2020iqtree} with ModelFinder \cite{kalyaanamoorthy2017modelfinder}, 1,000 ultrafast bootstraps
\cite{hoang2018ufboot} and 1,000 SH-aLRT replicates \cite{guindon2010phyml}. A culture, named species or complex was
\emph{monophyletic} when one branch of the unrooted tree separated exactly its sequences from all others, and
\emph{strongly non-monophyletic} when a branch that contradicts it had SH-aLRT $\ge80$ and ultrafast bootstrap
$\ge95$. Each group was tested with all sequences and with the divergent sequences removed.

\emph{Artifact check.} Each divergent sequence was aligned to the most similar main-type sequence of its own
culture. A deletion was \emph{long} when it reached, in its region, the 99th percentile of the longest deletion
between main-type sequences of one culture, with a floor of 3~nt: \ArtLConserved{}~nt in the SSU, 5.8S and LSU, and
\ArtLSpacer{}~nt in ITS1 and ITS2. A divergent sequence \emph{recurred} when a sequence within 1.0\% of it in full
ITS occurred under another culture label, i.e., in another isolate. The verdicts were fixed in advance: a recurring
sequence without long deletions is a paralog; a recurring sequence with long deletions is a recurrent deleted copy
(genomic, possibly a pseudogene); a single sequence with long deletions is a probable artifact; and a single
sequence without them is unresolved.

\emph{rRNA structure.} To ask whether divergent sequences keep the structure of functional rRNA, the SSU, 5.8S
and LSU of every sequence were aligned with Infernal \cite{nawrocki2013infernal} to the Rfam models of eukaryotic SSU
rRNA (RF01960), 5.8S rRNA (RF00002) and eukaryotic LSU rRNA (RF02543) \cite{ontiverospalacios2025rfam}, which
describe both the sequence and the secondary structure of each RNA family. A base pair of the consensus structure
was \emph{broken} in a sequence when the two bases could not pair (not a Watson--Crick or G$\cdot$U pair) or when
one of them was deleted. A sequence was \emph{degraded} in a region when its share of broken base pairs exceeded the
99th percentile among main-type sequences, and \emph{irregular} when its substitutions, or its insertion and
deletion events, counted against the most similar main-type sequence of its culture, exceeded the 99th percentile
among main-type sequences. The verdict for the short-5.8S class was fixed in advance: \emph{structurally intact} if
at most 10\% of its sequences were degraded in each region and its shared deletion removed only unpaired positions
of the consensus structure; \emph{structurally degraded}, as expected of pseudogenes, if at least 50\% were
degraded in some region or the shared deletion removed paired positions; and \emph{mixed} otherwise. To compare the
folding of the ITS2 between classes, the ITS2 of every sequence was folded with RNAfold \cite{lorenz2011viennarna};
this comparison was descriptive and entered no verdict.

\emph{Controls for the SSU-discordance flag.} After review, we asked what the first decision step detects, using
sequences whose origin is known: real main-type sequences, sequences given random errors, and chimeras built from
sequences of two species (Methods S8). This analysis is exploratory and changes no rule.

\subsection{Distances at each level of relatedness}\label{sec:distmethods}
To explore similarity thresholds without fixing any rule, we measured, for every main-type sequence, the distance to
its nearest main-type sequence in four classes: the same culture, another culture of the same species, another
species of the same genus, and another genus of the same order. We did this for the NS31--AM1 fragment, on which VTs
are defined, the whole SSU region, the full ITS and LR0R--FLR2, and we also measured the distances between the medoid
sequences of every pair of cultures within an order. The same distances were measured on two longer regions, the
Kr\"uger fragment and the whole sequence. Finally, we counted what the short-5.8S type does to clusters and to the
tree of each marker, and tested a sequence motif that marks it (Methods S8).

\subsection{The three naming systems are nested}\label{sec:nest}
The SSU gave a VT name to \VTAssigned{} of the \NCopiesRetained{} sequences. Counting each culture by its most common
VT, \VTSpeciesMulti{} of \VTSpeciesN{} named species span more than one VT, and \VTUnitsMulti{} of \VTUnitsN{} VTs

culture its own species for \LSUOwnSpecies{} of the \LSUOwnDenom{} named cultures whose species V18 holds

of those that get a name. V18 itself, however, holds two records for nearly every species (460 records under 234
species names: 215 names with two records, five with three or four, and 14 with one). Of the 220 names with two or

only 72\% of records returned their own species.

UNITE+INSD, which holds all public sequences, named \SHAssignedFull{} sequences (\SHAssignedFullPct{}), against

\subsection{Divergent sequences, and a paralog family in \spn{Rhizoglomus}}\label{sec:divergent}
The two divergent classes mislead each marker differently (Table~\ref{tab:divergent}). Short-5.8S sequences are well
represented in public data, as expected for the variable, non-tandem rRNA gene units of a single genome
\cite{maeda2018rdna}: their median best SH hit (\DivSHMedian{}) is closer than that of main-type sequences
(\MainSHMedian{}). Divergent sequences reach \DivSHs{} SHs, of which \ParalogSH{} are reached by no main-type
sequence (paralog SHs, \ParalogPlaceholder{} of them under placeholder names) and \DivSHOneMain{} by one main-type
sequence each (Table~\ref{tab:paralog}). The short-5.8S sequences of \spn{Septoglomus viscosum} are the only culture
sequences that reach VTX00409, about two percentage points closer than to VTX00063. In \spn{Gigaspora rosea}, four
cultures carry the same three or four rRNA gene types, identical across cultures and up to 6.2\% apart in the LSU,
each in its own SH named \spn{Gigaspora rosea}. These types are not confined to one isolate held in several
collections: in LongGloDB (Section~\ref{sec:lgdb}), INVAM cultures under other accession codes (\LgRoseaCultures{};
FL105 is INVAM's reference culture for the species) carry ASVs within \LgRoseaMaxITS{} of them in full ITS.

Under the artifact rule fixed in advance (Table~\ref{tab:artefacts}), \ArtSameParalog{} same-length sequences are

sequences of \spn{Rhizoglomus} represent a genomic paralog family rather than artifacts.

The SSU of the short-5.8S sequences kept its secondary structure: only \FnSSUDeg{} of the \FnShortN{} sequences
were degraded, and their share of broken base pairs did not differ from that of the main-type sequences of their culture
(median \FnSSUBrokenShort{} against \FnSSUBrokenBase{}; paired Wilcoxon test, $P=\FnSSUP{}$). However, their LSU carries far
more substitutions than the main-type sequences of their culture (median \FnLSUSubs{}\% against \FnLSUSubsBase{}\%
of aligned positions), but most of these keep the base pairing: \FnLSUDeg{} sequences (\FnLSUDegPct{}) were degraded,
and the share of broken base pairs differed little (\FnLSUBrokenShort{} against \FnLSUBrokenBase{}; $P=\FnLSUP{}$).
The 5.8S differs most from that of the main-type sequences of the same culture: \FnFiveIrr{} of the \FnShortN{}
sequences were irregular, and broken base pairs rose from \FnFiveBrokenBase{} to \FnFiveBrokenShort{} (median paired
difference \FnFiveDiff{} percentage points; $P$~\FnFiveP{}). In all \FnHairpinShort{} short-5.8S sequences of
\spn{Rhizoglomus}, the deletion removes three of the nine base pairs of a hairpin near the 3$'$ end of the 5.8S
(positions 121--130 of the Rfam consensus structure), whereas in the \FnUnpairedShort{} \spn{Septoglomus viscosum}
sequences it removes only positions that are unpaired in that structure. Under the rule fixed in advance, the
short-5.8S class is therefore \FnVerdictShort{}, as pseudogenes would be. The hairpin is shortened rather than
destroyed, however: the remaining six-pair stem is made only of Watson--Crick pairs in \FnStemAllWC{} of the
\FnHairpinShort{} sequences, and its new loop is UUCG in \FnLoopUUCG{}, UUUG in \FnLoopUUUG{} and CUCG in
\FnLoopCUCG{}; a UUCG loop closed by a C--G pair, as in most of these sequences, forms an unusually stable hairpin
\cite{tuerk1988cuucgg}. Two main-type sequences carry the same shortened hairpin, PX214776.1 (above) and PX214775.1
(\spn{Glomus} sp.\ 5089\_2351A), and both were among the sequences flagged by the artifact check. The same-length
sequences were \FnVerdictSame{}: none was degraded in any region. In the ITS2, the predicted folds of short-5.8S

energies ($\FnITSTwoMFEShort{}$ against $\FnITSTwoMFEBase{}$ kcal\,mol$^{-1}$ per nucleotide). Thus, the 5.8S of the
short type has lost part of one hairpin but keeps a stable, shortened stem.

Most short-5.8S sequences of \spn{Rhizoglomus} do not group with the main type of their own culture

sequences of \spn{Rhizoglomus intraradices} and \spn{Rhizoglomus proliferum}, two species that do not form a
complex, are closer to each other than to the main type of either species. The short type is therefore most simply
explained as a paralog that arose before these species separated and has been kept in their genomes since.

divergent class and PX214776.1, which carries the same 5.8S but was classified as main type (see above). In

identical to a main-type sequence of their species, and all but one of the rest, a sequence of \spn{Rhizoglomus}

Single-linkage clusters of all sequences of the \NSpecies{} named species at 98.5\% identity in the full ITS number

\subsection{No single identity threshold separates species}\label{sec:trees}
A main-type sequence lies about as close to its nearest culture-mate as to the nearest sequence of another culture
of its species (medians \ThrITSSameCulture{} and \ThrITSSameSpecies{} in the full ITS, \ThrLSUSameCulture{} and
\ThrLSUSameSpecies{} in the LSU; Figure~\ref{fig:thrnearest}). Congeners overlap this range in every region:
\ThrITSCongener{} of main-type sequences have a sequence of another species of their genus within the within-species
limit of the full ITS, \ThrLSUCongener{} within that of the LSU and \ThrSSUCongener{} within that of the SSU region.
Other genera stay outside it in every region except the NS31--AM1 fragment (\ThrVTOtherGenusSpread{} of sequences).
The database thresholds fall at different points on this scale. On the NS31--AM1 fragment, 97\% identity, the VT
threshold, joins \ThrVTCongenerDB{} of main-type sequences with a congener and \ThrVTOtherGenusDB{} with another
genus (\ThrVTOtherGenusN{} sequences, all but \ThrVTOtherGenusNotGiga{} in \spn{Cetraspora}, \spn{Gigaspora} and
\spn{Dentiscutata}). In the full ITS, the SH threshold of 98.5\% identity lies well within the within-species
limit: \ThrITSSameCultureAboveSH{} of main-type sequences are more than 1.5\% from every other main-type sequence of
their own culture, which helps explain why SHs split cultures. In the LSU, the 97\% threshold of V18 coincides with
the within-species limit (\LSUSpread{}).

Between pairs of cultures (Figure~\ref{fig:thrregions}), the SSU distance was a median of \ThrPairsRatio{} times
the ITS distance (pairs at least 2\% apart in ITS). Only \ThrPairsAbove{} of the \ThrPairsN{} pairs lay above half
the ITS distance, all of them cultures of one species with SSU distances of at most \ThrPairsAboveMaxSSU{}, so no
pair of cultures met the SSU-discordance test (SSU at least 1\% and at least half the ITS distance). That the test
spares real sequences says nothing of what it catches, so we also gave it sequences of known origin (Methods S8). It
flagged none of the \AcRealN{} main-type sequences scored with their culture withheld, although \AcRealSSUOne{} of
them lie at least 1\% from every other culture in the SSU region. Errors spread along a sequence were flagged once
they reached a few percent: \AcErrHalf{} of \AcErrN{} sequences with 0.5\% of positions changed, \AcErrTwo{} with
2\% and \AcErrEight{} with 8\%. Chimeras were almost never flagged: none of the \AcJunctionN{} that join the SSU of
one species to the ITS of another, because their SSU is close to that of its parent, and \AcSSUChimFlag{} of the
\AcSSUChimN{} that switch parents inside the SSU, because their nearest SSU usually belongs to a lineage whose ITS is
even more divergent. Thus, the first decision step flags SSU discordance and is sensitive to reads with many errors,
but it is not a chimera screen.

Taken together, the regions of the Kr\"uger fragment (from the SSUmAf site to LSUmBr; median \LongKrMedian{}~nt)
keep genomes and species apart better than the full ITS alone (Figure~\ref{fig:thrlong}). At 97\% identity,
\LongKrNSSplit{} of main-type sequences lie more than 3\% from every other sequence of their culture, against
\LongITSNESplit{} for the full ITS at 98.5\%, and outside the four complexes \LongKrNSJoinX{} have a congener within
3\%. The complexes stay joined at any cut-off that keeps genomes together: counted as separate species, they leave the
best single cut-off (\LongKrBestId{}) splitting \LongKrBestSplit{} of sequences from their culture and joining
\LongKrBestJoin{} to another species. The whole sequence (median \LongFullMedian{}~nt) behaves the same way at a
higher identity (best cut-off \LongFullBestId{}: \LongFullBestSplit{} split, \LongFullBestJoin{} joined). Thus, no
single identity threshold separates the species, although longer regions come closer than the ITS.

sequences lay inside its labelled species on all three markers; in the ITS, they lay \EkFxRuleDemotedITS{} from the
nearest culture of that species. A further \EkFxKnownDivergent{} sequences, all labelled \spn{Rhizoglomus irregulare}
or \spn{Rhizoglomus intraradices}, were classified as divergent sequences of the short-5.8S type.

For absent species in reference genera, the genus given was right for \EkGenusRight{} of the \EkFxSpGenusScored{}
sequences with a genus outcome, wrong for \EkGenusWrong{}, and missing for \EkGenusNone{}. For absent genera, any
genus given is wrong. With each such genus removed from the LSU reference, \EkLinGenusGiven{} of \EkLinScored{}
sequences (\EkFxLinGivenPct{}) were given a genus, most of them \EkFxLinGivenTop{}; with the LSU reference intact,
\EkFxLinGivenA{} (\EkFxLinGivenAPct{}). Thus, a genus missing from the LSU reference was often replaced by a related
genus, as in the LSU-removal test.

The short-5.8S motif occurs in \EkFxMotifRhiz{} EUKARYOME sequences labelled \spn{Rhizoglomus}. Of these,
\EkFxMotifRhizDiv{} were classified as divergent sequences, but \EkFxMotifRhizLineage{} were classified as new
lineages and \EkFxMotifRhizNewSp{} as new species in a known genus. Thus, in field sequences, the decision steps
recognized the paralog in about half of the cases, and the rest would have been counted as new taxa.

\section{Discussion}
% ============================================================================
Long-read sequences that link the SSU, ITS and LSU turn the correspondence between VTs, SHs and LSU species from a
guess into a measurement, and they show where each naming system misleads.

\subsection{The three systems are nested rather than equivalent}
SHs mostly fall within LSU species, which mostly fall within VTs. VT counts therefore underestimate the number of
species (at best, VTs separate \VTResolvedZero{} of \NSpecies{} species), and SH counts overestimate it, because the
rRNA gene copies of one genome can fall into up to \SHSplitMax{} SHs. Richness counted in SHs can thus be inflated by
variation within genomes. How much it is inflated in a field survey depends on which copies are amplified, detected,
clustered and kept, which we did not test. Grouping the SHs of one genome restores most of the species signal in our
cultures, but the groups were learned from these same cultures, and whether they transfer to field sequences is
untested. The 2.5-kb amplicons were introduced to bring the three marker regions into one dataset
\cite{kolarikova2021pacbio}; what was missing is a measured correspondence between the names, which linked
sequences supply.

\subsection{The short-5.8S copies are genomic; whether they are functional is open}
Variation among the rRNA gene copies of an AMF genome has long been known
\cite{pawlowska2004organization,vankuren2013rdna}, yet it has been largely overlooked in ecological and taxonomic
work \cite{stefani2025rdna}. What linked sequences add is a measure of how this variation misleads each marker.
Short-5.8S sequences look like new lineages in the LSU and form their own SHs and VTs, several of them under
placeholder names in UNITE. The short-5.8S test recognizes most of them from their sequence alone, but less often when
the whole species is absent from the reference, because no related culture with a 5.8S of the expected length is
then available for comparison.

In \spn{Rhizoglomus}, the short-5.8S sequences are not artifacts: the same 5.8S deletion recurs across isolates and
species and in independent libraries of the same culture, and on trees the short-5.8S sequences of different species
group together. The evidence is weaker for the other classes (Table~\ref{tab:classes}). The short-5.8S sequences of
\spn{Septoglomus viscosum} are genomic but known from one species only, and the same-length sequences outside
\spn{Gigaspora} are single sequences of unresolved origin. A long deletion that recurs should therefore be read as
part of a known genome rather than discarded as a singleton, whereas a divergent sequence seen only once remains an
open case.

Being part of a genome does not make the short-5.8S copies harmless. A species that carries them has two structural
variants of the marker, so the short-5.8S sequences form ITS clusters of their own (\ParITSNEShort{} at 98.5\%;
Table~\ref{tab:clusters}). Moreover, the ITS and LSU suggest a different history from the SSU: on the LSU tree, the
short-5.8S sequences of \spn{Rhizoglomus intraradices} and \spn{Rhizoglomus proliferum} group with \spn{Sclerocystis}
rather than with their own species. Because the class has a fixed signature, it can be flagged before clustering or
tree building: in \spn{Rhizoglomus}, a 13-nt motif in the 5.8S marks every short-5.8S sequence and no other. In UNITE,
the same motif sorts the SHs of \spn{Rhizoglomus} almost perfectly, and about one in five of these SHs consists only of
the short type, so the paralog probably inflates SH counts in the public database as it does in the cultures. Such a
flag has to be read together with the genus, because the motif is also the normal 5.8S of \spn{Oehlia diaphana} and
occurs in hundreds of unplaced Glomeraceae sequences, which only phylogenetic placement can sort out. The flag also
needs the end of the 5.8S: ITS2 amplicons made with fITS7 or ITS3 include it, but an ITS2 trimmed with ITSx no longer
does, so the flag applies to full-ITS and long-read sequences, and to ITS2 reads only before trimming. The motif was
written from the sequences it was tested on, except for the UNITE search. Species are therefore best counted from
main-type sequences, and the short type is best analysed as a locus of its own rather than mixed into a species tree.

Whether the short-5.8S copies are functional is a separate question. Their SSU keeps its base pairing, and their LSU,
although rich in substitutions, mostly keeps it too. The deletion shortens one hairpin of the 5.8S, so under the rule
we fixed in advance the class counts as structurally degraded, as pseudogenes would be. Yet the shortened stem
consists of Watson--Crick pairs and is mostly closed by a UUCG loop, a combination known for stable hairpins
\cite{tuerk1988cuucgg}. The predicted folding thus shows a structural change, not a loss of function: DNA alone cannot
distinguish a transcribed and processed copy from a silent one, and calling the class a pseudogene would go beyond
the evidence. Sequencing the rRNA or its precursors from the same cultures would show whether the short type ends up
in ribosomes. Same-length divergent sequences are more ambiguous: co-dominant types of one genome in \spn{Gigaspora},
and single sequences of unknown origin elsewhere. They should be flagged rather than explained away.

\subsection{No identity threshold delivers species}
The distances at each level of relatedness show why the three systems disagree (Figure~\ref{fig:thrnearest}): their
thresholds fall at different points on the scale of AMF variation. On the NS31--AM1 fragment, 97\% identity would join
almost every sequence with a congener (\ThrVTCongenerDB{}) and some with another genus, mostly in \spn{Cetraspora},
\spn{Gigaspora} and \spn{Dentiscutata}, which fits the coarse resolution of VTs (at most \VTResolvedZero{} of
\NSpecies{} species). In the full ITS, the 98.5\% threshold of SHs lies within the variation of a single culture, so
one genome often spans several SHs. In the LSU, the 97\% threshold of V18 matches the variation within species, but
\ThrLSUCongener{} of sequences have a congener within it. In no region does one threshold separate species, in line
with the absence of a barcode gap that Stefani et al.\ reported for each region \cite{stefani2025rdna}, so moving a
threshold only trades lumping against splitting. Together, the regions do better: on the Kr\"uger fragment, 97\%
identity splits few genomes and, outside the complexes, joins almost no species, and the whole sequence does the same
at a higher identity. What no cut-off removes are the species complexes and the short-5.8S paralog. Combined names,
with complexes reported as groups, avoid that trade-off where the markers allow it.

The SSU, by contrast, behaves predictably: between cultures, its distance was a median of \ThrPairsRatio{} times the
ITS distance, and no pair of cultures met the SSU-discordance test, so a sequence whose SSU differs by at least half as
much as its ITS is unlike any pair of cultured relatives. The test caught reads with errors spread along them once the
errors reached a few percent, but almost no chimeras, which keep an intact SSU from one parent. It therefore flags a
possible artifact; it does not replace a chimera check, and an unusual but genuine lineage with no cultured relative
could also trigger it.

\subsection{Where the markers set a limit to novelty detection}
SSU, ITS and LSU together cannot separate the members of \NComplexes{} species complexes, \NComplexSpecies{} species
in all: their closest sequences lie within each other's within-species limits on every marker. In the culture
tests, all errors that remain after the ITS rule fall within these complexes at the 99th percentile used throughout. This is a limit observed in
this reference, with complexes that the withheld species helped to define. As a prediction, the rule did well at the
level of species and less well at the level of sequences: applied within each round of the LSU-removal test, it
flagged every species that had errors and only \FwInsideNoFK{} that had none, but \FwCopyOutsideVTwo{} sequences
of \spn{Rhizoglomus irregulare} assigned to \spn{Rhizoglomus vesiculiferum} lay outside that species' limit. In the
LongGloDB test, whose predictions were fixed before the run, every false-known assignment was predicted; in the
EUKARYOME test, \EkFxOutside{} of \EkFK{} were not (see below). The same pair is the only exception at other percentiles: with the rule and the complexes
both at the 95th or 97.5th percentile, \spn{Rhizoglomus irregulare} and \spn{Rhizoglomus vesiculiferum} are no longer
a complex, and the sequences of \spn{Rhizoglomus irregulare} assigned to \spn{Rhizoglomus vesiculiferum} become
errors outside a complex (Table~\ref{tab:percentile}). Whether these two names can be separated with rRNA genes is
therefore the open question.

Within a complex, a species assignment is reliable only as a group, and novelty cannot be detected: an undescribed
relative within the limit of a known species cannot be distinguished from that species with these markers. The
alternative interpretation is that some of these names do not represent separate lineages, a question for
taxonomists; \spn{Diversispora aestuarii} and \spn{Diversispora varaderana} differ by 0.0\% in the SSU and 1.1\% in
the LSU. The rule depends on the data and will change as cultures are added: one further pair (\NearMissPair{})
misses the \NearMissMarker{} limit by \NearMissPoints{} percentage points.

The EUKARYOME test exposed two gaps in the rule. First, the rule compares a sequence with named species only. The
\EkFxGenusOnlyInside{} sequences labelled \EkFxGenusOnlyTaxa{} lie inside reference cultures identified only to genus,
and they took the names of the named species with which those cultures share a VT; the LSU clade of the unnamed
cultures could not object, because it is linked to no species. A reference culture without a species name is
therefore not neutral: it lends its sequences to its named relatives. Such cultures should be identified, or a match
to a name held only by unnamed cultures should block an assignment to species; we have not tested the second option.
Second, LSU placement put the \EkFxNotInside{} sequences of \EkFxNotInsideTaxa{} into the clade of
\EkFxNotInsideAssigned{}, although they lie outside its LSU limit. If the EUKARYOME labels are right, close relatives
of reference species were misidentified at a higher rate (\EkFxSpPct{} of sequences) than in the LSU-removal test
(\MfourFKvTwoPct{}), although averaged over all absent taxa the rate was only \EkFKPct{}.

\subsection{Novelty depends on what the reference already contains}
With only the cultures withheld, LSU placement looks like a strong detector of new species, but much of that strength
comes from the withheld species still being present in the LSU reference: its clade holds no culture, so its
sequences are flagged as new. Phylogenetic placement was proposed because matching against a database cannot place
sequences of taxa that are absent from it \cite{delavaux2021lsu,delavaux2022lsu}. This holds at the level of clades,
but the LSU-removal test shows its limit at the level of species: with the species removed, a sequence is placed on
its nearest named relative and then named after it, and with a genus removed, it is placed on the sister genus
(\GenusWrongN{} \spn{Rhizoglomus} sequences assigned to \spn{Sclerocystis}). An undescribed species is absent from
every reference. Removing it from the LSU reference is therefore closer to the real situation than leaving it in, but
not equal to it: MaarjAM and UNITE were left intact, and many VTs are built from environmental sequences, so a species
that is undescribed but already sequenced in the field can still have a VT of its own. The VT accounted for the
novelty of \MfourNOne{} of the \MfourHeldN{} sequences, so for a species never sequenced the false-known rate may be
higher than reported, by up to about \MfourNOnePct{} of sequences. The ITS distance rule does not depend on external
databases and closes part of the gap, but not where the ITS itself overlaps. Pooled over the test species,
false-known assignments rose from \MfourControlPct{} with the LSU reference intact to \MfourFKvOnePct{} with the
species removed from it, and fell to \MfourFKvTwoPct{} with the ITS rule, with wide intervals because
\CiMfourVTwoSpN{} species accounted for all the errors. Each of these rates belongs to its reference condition, and
none is the error rate for an undescribed species.

At the rank of genus, the same mechanism produces errors that look like answers. With a genus removed, its sequences
were not classified as a new lineage but given the sister genus, and nothing in the assignment marked them. A genus
that rests only on the nearest represented clade should therefore be treated with caution when the sequence lies far
from every reference culture. A flag for such assignments, for instance based on the distance to the nearest culture
of the assigned genus, is worth testing but was not part of the steps fixed here. The EUKARYOME test shows the scale
of the problem: with their genus removed from the LSU reference, \EkFxLinGivenPct{} of the sequences of absent genera
were given a genus, against \EkFxLinGivenAPct{} with the genus present.

\subsection{Relation to earlier work on the same cultures}\label{sec:stefani}
Stefani et al.\ \cite{stefani2025rdna} sequenced the rRNA genes used here (\StefaniCultures{} cultures of
\StefaniSpecies{} species) and compared them with three protein-coding genes (glomalin, H$^+$-ATPase and RPB1),
sequenced for \StefaniPCCultures{} cultures of \StefaniPCSpecies{} species. They found no barcode gap in any of the
regions, so that no single distance threshold identifies AMF, and concluded that rRNA genes remain the practical
choice because their primers work across all lineages. Our results agree where they overlap: the complexes are pairs
whose closest sequences lie within each other's limits in every rRNA gene region. Two of their observations follow
from the short-5.8S paralog. The rRNA genes of \spn{Rhizoglomus vesiculiferum} and \spn{Rhizoglomus proliferum} were
paraphyletic in their trees, nested in \spn{Rhizoglomus irregulare} and \spn{Rhizoglomus intraradices}; on our
concatenated tree, these four species become clades once the divergent sequences are set aside
(Section~\ref{sec:divergent}). Moreover, their largest within-species distances on the Kr\"uger fragment were in the
species that carry the short type (\spn{R.\ irregulare} 7.7\%, \spn{R.\ vesiculiferum} 10.2\%, \spn{R.\ proliferum}
and \spn{R.\ intraradices} up to 11\%) and in \spn{Glomus chinense} and \spn{Glomus rugosae} (7.4\% and 8\%), members
of one of our complexes. Their distances are maximum pairwise distances over all sequences, with every gap counted,
whereas ours are distances to the nearest sequence, so the numbers are not directly comparable. The questions also
differ: they asked whether a distance threshold identifies a species within one marker, whereas we asked how the
three naming systems relate and how far novelty can be detected once divergent copies are accounted for.

Their protein-coding sequences are public (glomalin PV808915--PV809055, RPB1 PV809056--PV809196, H$^+$-ATPase
PV809197--PV809333) and could settle the status of the complexes: if those genes separate \spn{Diversispora
aestuarii} from \spn{Diversispora varaderana}, the complex is a limit of the rRNA genes, and if not, it is a question
of naming. We compared them (Table~\ref{tab:pcgenes}). At the thresholds Stefani et al.\ proposed, the three genes
separate almost every pair of congeners that the rRNA genes separate (\PcBaseline{}; \PcBaselinePct{}). Of the
\PcComplexPairsAll{} pairs of species within complexes, \PcComplexPairsN{} have protein-coding records for both
species. Of these, \PcComplexAboveN{} exceed the threshold on one gene each (\PcComplexAbove{}), but
\PcComplexCleanText{} of the \PcComplexComparisons{} comparisons exceeds the variation within the species
themselves. For the pairs with records, the complexes are therefore not a limit of the rRNA genes alone: on these
genes, too, their members lie as close as cultures of one species, and whether they are separate species is a
question for taxonomy rather than for the choice of marker. The pairs without records, and other genes or criteria,
could still separate them. Linking the records through culture names also showed that the RPB1 and H$^+$-ATPase
deposits exchange the species names of three cultures: \PcConflictDetail{}. Each of these records lies
\PcConflictNearest{} from records of its culture's species, so the sequences are correct and the names are not; we
left the \PcConflicts{} records out.

\subsection{Why the rules are not calibrated per species}
A calibrated rule for deciding when an ITS sequence is new exists for fungi in general \cite{obrien2026flag}. Here, the
limit of the ITS distance rule is a quantile of the reference, the 99th percentile of the distance between cultures
of one species, and within the reference it behaved as intended: it changed \ITSDemotedKnown{} of the \KnownN{}
sequences of known species to `new' (\ITSDemotedKnownPct{}). It is nonetheless an empirically chosen decision
threshold, not a calibrated error rate: in cultures from another collection, it changed \CiLgDemotedPct{} of the ASVs
of known species and prevented no error (Section~\ref{sec:lgdb}). The 99th percentile was fixed before the test species
were run. The 95th percentile would have reduced false-known assignments further (\PctNinetyFiveFKPct{} against
\PctNinetyNineFKPct{} with the species removed from the LSU reference) at the cost of changing
\PctNinetyFiveKnownDemotedPct{} of the sequences of known species instead of \PctNinetyNineKnownDemotedPct{}
(Table~\ref{tab:percentile}), a trade-off that users can set, and should set with their collection in mind. The other
rules, such as the change to `new' when a VT or LSU name is unmatched, are not calibrated, because the reference holds
too few cultures per species to fit a threshold for each, and because the problems peculiar to AMF, divergent copies
and species complexes, have to be handled before a calibration can mean anything.

\subsection{Independent tests}
On \LgTestCultures{} cultures from three other laboratories, with the predictions fixed before the run, the rules
assigned a species absent from the reference to a known species only where the complex rule had predicted it:
\spn{Gigaspora gigantea} KS302, which lies inside \spn{Gigaspora rosea} on every marker. The test is easier than ours
in one respect and harder in two. Its absent species are distant from those of the reference, so it says little about
the sister-species case, which occurred once. Its amplicons start inside the VT fragment, which leaves the VT search
silent unless coverage is counted over the part that a sequence spans, and a genus taken from the VT alone was then
wrong when the true genus was absent from the reference (\spn{Racocetra} given \spn{Cetraspora}). Finally, its
cultures come from other collections, some of which lie farther from ours in the ITS than our cultures of one species
lie from each other: the ITS distance rule, fitted at the 99th percentile on one collection, changed \LgNSixDemoted{}
of the \LgKnownN{} ASVs of known species to `new' and prevented no false-known assignment.

The EUKARYOME test complements it with field and reference sequences of many more taxa, including close relatives of
reference species, but its names are a classification rather than culture names. Here the ITS distance rule did
prevent errors (\EkFxRulePrevented{}), at the cost of \EkFxRuleCost{} sequences of reference species that it changed
to `new', all of which lay outside their labelled species on at least one marker. Whether those sequences are
mislabelled or show real variation within species cannot be decided from EUKARYOME. In either case, the limit of the
rule should be fitted on more than one collection before the rule is used on field data.

\subsection{Implications for surveys}
We recommend naming taxa at the level that the markers support: report VTs, SHs grouped by genome and LSU species
clades, with species groups where the markers cannot decide, and do not expect any identity threshold to deliver
species on its own. Screen for the short-5.8S type before counting taxa or building trees, for instance with the motif in
\spn{Rhizoglomus}: in EUKARYOME, the decision steps recognized only \EkFxMotifRhizDiv{} of the \EkFxMotifRhiz{}
sequences of \spn{Rhizoglomus} that carry it, and classified \EkFxMotifRhizLineage{} as new lineages. Treat
paralog SHs and VTs, such as SH1337353 and VTX00409, as parts of known genomes. Identify reference cultures to
species, or keep cultures without a species name from lending species names to the sequences that match them. Base
claims of novelty on evidence that does not come
from the database that defines what is known, report error rates together with the reference they were measured
against, and interpret names according to a stated taxonomy \cite{tedersoo2024amf}, because outdated names in a database look like errors of
the marker \cite{dasilva2024families}. Treat the SSU-discordance flag as a check for noisy reads rather than for
chimeras, and a genus given to a sequence that lies far from every reference culture as provisional. Replication
across independent sequencing libraries is still needed for environmental data.

\subsection{Limitations}
Apart from the LongGloDB and EUKARYOME tests, all results come from one collection of cultured species, and these
cultures were used to develop the methods, so the results are development estimates. The LongGloDB test rests on
\LgNovelCultures{} cultures of absent species, covers only distant novelty, and used amplicons on which the VT
search, as specified, is silent. The EUKARYOME test covers close relatives, but only \EkNovelSpN{} of its sequences
belong to absent species in reference genera; its names are a classification, less reliable at species level than
culture names, so some of its errors may be errors of the labels; and EUKARYOME is curated by one of us. The 99th percentile behind the ITS rule and the complexes was chosen after the distances in these cultures had
been seen (Table~\ref{tab:percentile} shows the effect of other choices), and the complexes were derived from all
cultures, including the withheld species; applied within each round, the rule anticipated the species at risk but not
every misidentified sequence. Sequences, cultures and species are nested, and intervals that resample species are wide
(Table~\ref{tab:intervals}). Culture names are treated as correct, although some are doubtful:
\spn{Microdominikia litorea} 5274, some \spn{Gigaspora rosea} cultures, and \spn{Septoglomus} sp.\ C4 and
5070\_5024A, whose LSU matches \spn{Microviscospora peruviscosa}. One of the \ITSDemotedKnown{} sequences of known
species changed to `new' by the ITS rule comes from \spn{Microdominikia litorea} 5274, and the reference's
\spn{Ambispora leptoticha}, ON205A, is \spn{Ambispora gerdemannii} in INVAM, so some apparent errors may be errors of
the names, and agreement with a deposited name does not prove the species. Recurrence across culture labels can
overstate independence, because one isolate may be held by several collections under different codes, and only
\ClAllLibrepTested{} of the \ClAllCopies{} divergent sequences come from cultures with replicate libraries. MaarjAM and
UNITE stayed intact in every test, and the LSU-removal test removed species from the LSU reference only. About a
quarter of the V18 species names with several records (51 in the placement audit) do not form exclusive clades on its
backbone tree. The structure analysis reads DNA: a copy that keeps its base pairing need not be transcribed or end up
in ribosomes, and a copy that loses a pair need not be silent. The distances at each level of relatedness are
exploratory and fix no rule. Replication on environmental sequencing libraries is outstanding.

\section{Conclusions}
% ============================================================================
Long-read rRNA gene sequences that link the SSU, ITS and LSU provide an empirical bridge between the three AMF naming
systems and show where each one misleads. Detecting novelty is only as reliable as the reference databases are
complete, and it cannot go below the resolution of the markers: with the withheld species removed from the LSU
reference, \MfourFKvTwoPct{} of their sequences were still assigned to a known species, all of them within species
pairs that SSU, ITS and LSU cannot separate. As a prediction, the complex rule flagged the species at risk, and in
cultures from other collections the only species misidentified as known was the one it predicted, but it did not
anticipate every misidentified sequence. In EUKARYOME, it missed most errors, which arose through reference cultures
identified only to genus. Divergent copies must be recognized before taxa are counted: in
\spn{Rhizoglomus}, the short-5.8S type is a paralog family shared across species, whereas the origin of other
divergent sequences is less certain. Species that the markers cannot separate, and that the available protein-coding
sequences do not separate either, should be reported as species complexes.

\subsection*{Methods S1. Marker fragments}
ITSxRust anchored each read with one nhmmer pass (HMMER~3 \cite{eddy2011hmmer}, ITSx fungal profile
\cite{bengtsson2013itsx}) and delimited the SSU region, ITS1, 5.8S, ITS2, full ITS and LSU region. We used tolerant
anchoring thresholds (E-value $\le10^{-3}$, anchor score $\ge15$), because anchoring only delimits regions. A panel
of seven fragments (Table~\ref{tab:stage1}) was cut at the original primer sites, so that each fragment covers the
region on which its database was built. The panel used the primers of the SSU virtual taxa (NS31, AM1 and AML2
\cite{simon1992specific,helgason1998ploughing,lee2008improved,opik2010maarjam}), the SSU V4 primers WANDA and
AMV4.5NF/AMDGR \cite{dumbrell2011distinct,sato2005}, the LSU primers LR0R \cite{bunyard1994morchella,delavaux2021lsu}
and FLR2 \cite{trouvelot1999visualization}, the D2 primers FLR3 and FLR4 \cite{gollotte2004diversity}, and the outer
pair AML1 and LSUmBr \cite{lee2008improved,kruger2009dna} of the long amplicon \cite{kolarikova2021pacbio}, with
LSUmBr as the degenerate consensus of its five variants \cite{schlaeppi2016profiling}. Primers were located with an
IUPAC-aware infix alignment (edlib \cite{sosic2017edlib}) that tolerated edits up to 15\% of the primer length;
fragments were primer-trimmed, and coordinates are 1-based, inclusive and in rRNA-sense orientation. Every primer
sequence was checked against published sources (\path{analysis/primer_check.tsv}). A primer was searched for only in
the part of the read where it can lie (SSU primers before the ITS, LSU primers after it, plus 50~nt). A fragment is
\emph{open} when a primer site lies beyond the end of the sequence, and \emph{missing} when the primer is absent
although the sequence covers its expected position. Sequences were also screened for repeats (concatemers) with the
concatemer screen of version~0.3.1 of our software. No fragment crosses an ITSxRust anchor, and no sequence was
flagged as a repeat.

\subsection*{Methods S2. Culture reference}
The names of the deposit are those of the culture tables of Stefani et al.\ (their Tables S2 and S3). Their
\StefaniPCSpecies{} protein-coding species are phylogenetic species, which split \spn{Septoglomus} sp.\ into three;
their \StefaniSpecies{} rRNA gene species are not itemized, and we used the names of the deposit, with the species
deposited in \spn{Rhizophagus} given in \spn{Rhizoglomus} \cite{sieverding2015rhizoglomus}. A culture is an organism
name plus a culture label. The reference was built by \path{freeze_ref.py} from the fragment tables and a file of
edits. Every edit first stated what the data had to look like (organism, label, number of sequences, VT, sequence
class, 5.8S length), and a failed check stopped the build before anything was written. Each version recorded the
checksums of its input and output files and a fingerprint. The edits leading to version~4 (fingerprint
\RefFingerprint) are listed in Table~\ref{tab:edits}. The sequence classes follow the 5.8S: sequences whose 5.8S lies
more than 3\% from that of the culture's medoid were flagged, and the flagged sequences were split into short-5.8S
sequences, at least 3~bp shorter than the culture's main type, and same-length sequences. Where the medoid fell inside
the short-5.8S group, the flags came out inverted, and the classes were swapped (Table~\ref{tab:edits}).

\subsection*{Methods S3. Searches and near-best names}
The VT search used the MaarjAM gDAT snapshot of July 2020 \cite{opik2010maarjam}, the SH search UNITE release 10.0 of
19 February 2025 \cite{nilsson2019unite,unite2025release}, and the LSU search the Delavaux V18 database of May 2025
\cite{delavaux2021lsu,delavaux2022lsu,delavaux2024lsu} (repository commit \texttt{f4a0014}). Searches used BLAST+
\cite{camacho2009blast} with the settings in Table~\ref{tab:arms}. Many V18 records carry terminal N padding, so
coverage did not count reference Ns; without that correction, 95 of the 460 records would fail the coverage test even
against themselves. Hits to any accession of the culture reference were disregarded. The name of a culture in a
system is the most common name of its main-type sequences (ties went to the higher median identity). The names of a
sequence depend only on its fragment, the database and the disregarded accessions, not on culture names. The
comparison of the names (the crosswalk) was run before the last two \spn{Septoglomus} edits (Table~\ref{tab:edits}),
so its report on cultures was replayed on the final labels with the stored names of each sequence
(\path{crosswalk_replay.py}), after every stored name had been checked against evidence captured on the final
reference: in all three systems and for all \NCopiesRetained{} sequences, the stored identity equaled that of the
highest-scoring hit passing the threshold, and the best name was present at that identity.

Each sequence received a set of near-best names instead of a single best hit. The anchor was the sequence's best hit
by bit score, and the set held every name within $\delta$ identity points of the anchor that also passed the identity
threshold. Names that split the main-type sequences of one culture at $\delta=0$ were merged when the closest
sequences across the split were within 1\% in the SSU region; this limit prevents the two lineages of a mixed culture
from being merged. $\delta$ was set to the smallest margin at which the main-type sequences of at least 99\% of
cultures share a name; for VTs, it was also at least the 95th percentile of the SSU differences within cultures. SHs
and LSU names have no such floor, because their variation within genomes is wide and uneven (95th percentile: ITS
8.6\%, LR0R--FLR2 3.9\%), and the merged names absorb that variation instead (Table~\ref{tab:sets}).

\subsection*{Methods S4. LSU placement}
The V18 reference sequences were realigned with MAFFT \cite{katoh2013mafft}, and branch lengths and GTR+$\Gamma_4$
parameters were fitted on the published topology with RAxML-NG \cite{kozlov2019raxmlng}. Query sequences were added
with \texttt{--addfragments --keeplength} and placed with EPA-ng \cite{barbera2019epang}. The compatible edges of a
query carry at least 95\% of its likelihood weight. Edges inside a maximal clade bearing a single V18 species name
were collapsed into one name (the LSU species clade). Where a species name is not monophyletic on the backbone tree,
its separate components stayed separate names, and edges that carry several species names stayed individual names.

\subsection*{Methods S5. Combining the names and the decision steps}
A name was linked to the species and genera of the training cultures whose sets of near-best names hold it. In each
system, a sequence is \emph{matched} when its names belong to training cultures, \emph{unmatched} when it has a name
but no training culture holds it, and \emph{silent} when it has no name. Genera were intersected over the matched
systems, and species over the matched systems whose names are linked to named species. The repository's codes for
the decision steps are: SSU discordance (possible artifact), A1; divergent sequence, D1 (short 5.8S) and D2 (paralog
name), with D3 for a same-length 5.8S that is only flagged; near a VT threshold, B1; known species or species group,
K1 (all matched systems agree and neither the VT nor the LSU name is unmatched); new species in a known genus, N1 (VT
unmatched), N2 (LSU unmatched), N3 (no species remains while a genus does) and N6 (the ITS distance rule);
discordant, X1 (the matched systems share no genus); and new lineage, N4 (no system matched) and N5 (as N4, with the
best VT also below the near-threshold band). The comparators of D1 are the main-type sequences of the nearest training
culture; the support of D2 is the number of other training cultures that reach the name only through short-5.8S
sequences. Divergent sequences are named by the VT alone. A sequence is scored against what the reference contains:
main-type sequences of species held by another training culture should receive a known species or species group
that contains their species; main-type sequences of withheld species should not receive a known species (otherwise a
false-known assignment); and divergent sequences should be recognized as divergent or, for same-length sequences, at
least not be considered new.

\subsection*{Methods S6. Withholding tests and the choice of percentile}
Every quantity derived from cultures was fitted on the training cultures of each round only: merged names, $\delta$,
the links from names to species, the comparators of D1, the support of D2, the pools of nearest sequences and the
limit of the ITS distance rule. The test with withheld cultures had 148 rounds, each removing one physical culture
label together with all lineages sharing it; the test with withheld species had 50 rounds, one per named species
with all its cultures removed plus one per culture not named to species. In these tests, MaarjAM, UNITE and V18 stayed
fixed. In the LSU-removal test, V18 was realigned without the target's records, the model refitted and all sequences
placed again (Table~\ref{tab:m4folds}). The second version of the protocol added the ITS distance rule (N6) and
changed nothing else; \spn{Glomus chinense} and \spn{Glomus rugosae} were used to develop that rule and are reported
as development rounds. The test species (the `first-outcome' rounds in the repository) are all other species: the
\MfourFolds{} whose V18 records were removed, and \spn{\MfourAbsentTarget}, which V18 does not hold by name, reported
apart. The same protocol had \GenusFolds{} genus rounds, in which all cultures of a genus and the genus's V18 records
were withheld together.

\emph{The complex rule within each round} (added after review; \path{analysis/foldwise_complexes.py}). For each
species round of the LSU-removal test, the cultures withheld in that round (\path{holdout_plan.json}) were removed,
the within-species limit of each marker was recomputed from the remaining main-type sequences of named species as in
\path{species_groups.py}, and each main-type sequence of the withheld species was compared with the deduplicated
main-type sequences of every remaining named species. A sequence lay inside a species when its distance to the
nearest sequence of that species was at most the limit on all three markers, the rule used for the locked
predictions. With nothing withheld, the recomputed limits equal those of the main text. The withheld sequences served
only as queries. False-known assignments per species were taken from Table~\ref{tab:m4folds}, and matched to
individual sequences with the per-sequence results of the run.

\emph{Intervals} (\path{analysis/intervals.py}). The withheld-culture and withheld-species tests were rescored from
the frozen tables of the decision steps with the frozen ITS distance rule, refitted per round, and every count was
checked against the values reported here. For each rate, species (or cultures) were resampled with replacement 10,000
times (seed 20261015) and the rate pooled over the resampled units; the intervals are the 2.5th and 97.5th
percentiles. For the LSU-removal test, intervals over species come from the per-species counts and intervals over cultures
from the per-sequence results, with a random stream of their own, so that adding them changed no other interval.

To test the choice of percentile, the ITS distance rule and the complexes were set together at the 95th and 97.5th
percentiles (Table~\ref{tab:percentile}): the rule was re-applied to the stored results of the decision steps, with
the limit of each round refitted on its training cultures (\path{percentile_sensitivity.py}). Percentiles follow
Python's exclusive quantile method throughout, as in the complex rule (\path{species_groups.py}).

\subsection*{Methods S7. The independent tests}
LongGloDB v1 holds \LgTotal{} ASVs. We left out the \LgStefani{} ASVs that carry the laboratory codes of the Stefani
et al.\ cultures (STFP and STFI): \LgStefaniIdentical{} of them are identical in full ITS to a sequence in our
reference, so they are the same cultures processed again. A further \LgCodeShared{} cultures share a culture code
with a Stefani label (\LgCodeConflicts{} of them under another species name) and were left out too. This overlap was
established with \path{longglodb_overlap.py}. The protocol and its two amendments (\path{PROTOCOL_LGDB.md}) fixed the
outcomes, conditions and analyses, and the predictions of the complex rule were fixed by their checksums before any
test sequence was searched, placed or scored. The frozen code of the second protocol version was used unchanged. All
\NCultures{} reference cultures were used for training and every test culture was excluded, so no test sequence
entered merges, margins, name links, comparators or the limit of the ITS distance rule. Test sequences were cut by
the same fragment step as the culture sequences, and their VT and SH evidence was computed with the same filtering
against the same releases. In condition~A, the test fragments were placed on the intact V18; in condition~B, each of
the \LgFolds{} absent species, all of which V18 holds by name, was removed from V18 in its own round, as in the
LSU-removal test. The second analysis (amendment~2) measured VT coverage over the part of each MaarjAM reference that
the query spans.

The EUKARYOME test (\path{PROTOCOL_EUK.md} and its note~1, in \path{benchmarks/eukaryome}) ran the same frozen code,
through a wrapper that replaces only the reader of the test set, the plan of condition~B and the culture identifiers,
with the same parity checks. The name table, roles and removals were made by \path{eukaryome_overlap.py}, and the
sequences were cut by the fragment step with the options used for the culture sequences. The predictions were
computed by the same script as for LongGloDB, with the within-species limits passed at the definition of the main
text (ITS \EkTITS{}, LSU \EkTLSU{}, SSU region \EkTSSU{}), so the two definitions noted for LongGloDB do not arise.
Condition~B removed \EkRounds{} sets of V18 records, one per absent species or genus that V18 holds, with every record
of a genus removed in its round.
The run outputs, without sequences, are in \path{benchmarks/m4_external_holdout/runs_euk}.

\subsection*{Methods S8. Trees, artifact check, rRNA structure and distances}
The protocol (\path{benchmarks/copy_checks/PROTOCOL_COPY_CHECKS.md}) was fixed on 11 October 2026 and the structure
analysis (\path{ADDENDUM1_FUNCTION.md}) on 12 October 2026, each before its results were computed.

\emph{Phylogenetic trees.} The sequences of each order (Archaeosporales 30, Paraglomerales 28, Diversisporales 245
and Glomerales 600) were analysed separately, because the ITS cannot be aligned across orders and every culture and
species lies within one order. MAFFT v7.505 L-INS-i (\texttt{--localpair --maxiterate 1000}) was used without
trimming; the concatenated alignment joins the three alignments with one partition per region. IQ-TREE 2.3.6 was run
with ModelFinder (\texttt{-m MFP}; for the concatenated alignment, one model per partition with edge-linked
proportional branch lengths), 1,000 ultrafast bootstraps (\texttt{-B 1000}), 1,000 SH-aLRT replicates
(\texttt{--alrt 1000}), near-zero branches collapsed (\texttt{-czb}) and seed 20261011. Each tip is one distinct
sequence and stands for every identical sequence. A group is tested on a tree when it has at least two sequences. It
is \emph{unresolved} when no branch separates it and none contradicts it (it sits in a polytomy, or shares an
identical sequence with another group); a branch that only joins identical sequences has no support value and never
counts as strong. For a group that is not monophyletic, its \emph{escapees} are its sequences outside the largest
clade made only of its sequences. All 1,436 tests of monophyly were recomputed independently with Bio.Phylo, with the
same result.

\emph{Artifact check.} Each sequence was aligned to the nearest main-type sequence of its own culture (distance over
the whole sequence; never itself) by global alignment with free end gaps (Biopython \texttt{PairwiseAligner}; match 2,
mismatch $-3$, gap opening $-5$ and extension $-2$, the BLASTN defaults). Gaps that touch either end of the alignment
reflect differences in read length and were ignored. Each internal deletion was assigned to the region of the
comparator (SSU region, ITS1, 5.8S, ITS2, LSU region) that holds most of its bases. Two sequences are of the same type
when their full-ITS distance is at most 1.0\%, and the two lineages of a mixed label count as one isolate. Sensitivity
analyses fixed in advance: same type at 0.5\% and 2.0\%, and long deletions read as at least 10~nt in every region.

\emph{Controls for the SSU-discordance flag} (added after review; \path{stage3/artifact_control.py}, results in
\path{benchmarks/copy_checks/artifact_control}). The first decision step was re-implemented exactly as frozen: the
nearest main-type sequences of other cultures in the SSU region, with ties, and the flag when the SSU distance is at
least 1\% and at least half the full-ITS distance to every tied sequence. Queries: every main-type sequence, with its
culture label withheld; one main-type sequence per culture label, with random errors at 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 5 and 8\% of
positions (60\% substitutions, 20\% insertions, 20\% deletions) applied to the SSU region and the full ITS alike; and
chimeras of main-type sequences of two different named species, 150 with parents in different genera and 150 with
congeneric parents of each kind, either joining the SSU region of one to the full ITS of the other or switching
parents at a random point in the middle 80\% of the SSU region. Each query was scored with the culture labels of its
parents withheld. Seed 20261015; 95\% intervals are Wilson intervals.

\emph{rRNA structure.} Infernal 1.1.5 \texttt{cmalign} was run with truncated alignments allowed. The groups were the
short-5.8S sequences, the same-length sequences, the main-type sequences with a long deletion in the SSU region, 5.8S
or LSU region (\emph{flagged main}), and all other main-type sequences with a main-type culture-mate (the baseline,
which set the 99th percentiles). The paired comparison set each divergent sequence against the mean of the main-type
sequences of its culture (two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test). The 5.8S deletions of the short-5.8S sequences were
located on the RF00002 consensus. The ITS2 was folded with ViennaRNA 2.7.2 \texttt{RNAfold} (default parameters,
37\,$^\circ$C): minimum free energy per nucleotide, and the base-pair distance between the structure of a sequence and
that of its comparator.

\emph{Distances.} For pairs of cultures, each culture was represented by its medoid main-type sequence, the sequence
with the smallest summed full-ITS distance to the other main-type sequences of its culture. The within-species limit
of the NS31--AM1 fragment (\ThrVTSpread{}) was computed as for the three markers of the complex rule. The 28
Paraglomerales sequences lack the NS31--AM1 fragment and were left out of that region.

\emph{Longer regions.} The Kr\"uger fragment was cut from each sequence from the SSUmAf1 site \cite{kruger2009dna}
(found with at most one mismatch in all 903 sequences) to the end of the sequence at LSUmBr, so the last stretch of
the SSUmAf--LSUmAr amplicon is missing; the whole sequence runs from AML1 to LSUmBr. Distances, classes and limits are
as above. For a cut-off $t$, a main-type sequence is split from its culture if it lies more than $t$ from every other
sequence of its culture, and joined to another species if a sequence of another species of its genus lies within
$t$; the partners of the four complexes are counted both ways (Figure~\ref{fig:thrlong}). Script:
\path{stage3/copy_long_fragments.py}.

\emph{What the short type does.} Short-5.8S sequences were counted by their 5.8S sequence: the short-5.8S class and
every sequence whose 5.8S is identical to one of them (this adds PX214776.1). All retained sequences of the named
species were clustered by single linkage within each order at 98.5\% and 97\% identity on the full ITS, the Kr\"uger
fragment and the whole sequence, once with every sequence and once with main-type sequences only
(Table~\ref{tab:clusters}). On the Glomerales tree of each marker, rooted on a \spn{Funneliformis} tip, we recorded the
clades made only of short-5.8S sequences, their support, the taxa they join and the main-type taxa of their sister
group. Script: \path{stage3/copy_paralog_effects.py}.

\emph{A motif for the short type.} The motif YTYGGRGARTATG covers the short-type hairpin of every short-5.8S sequence
of \spn{Rhizoglomus}. It was written from these sequences and tested on the same sequences, so it shows that such a
flag is possible, not its error rate. We searched for it in the 5.8S and in the whole sequence of all \MotifCopies{}
retained sequences, and located the ITS2 primers fITS7 \cite{ihrmark2012new} and ITS3 \cite{white1990amplification} in
the 5.8S, allowing up to two mismatches (ITS3 matched every \spn{Rhizoglomus} sequence with one or two). Script:
\path{stage3/short58_motif.py}; results in \path{benchmarks/copy_checks/short58_motif}. As an independent test, we
searched for the motif in all \UniteSeqs{} sequences of the UNITE+INSD set of 19 February 2025
\cite{unite2025release}, which holds none of the culture sequences. A hit counted as in the 5.8S when fITS7 (up to two
mismatches) started 30--45~nt before it, as in the culture sequences (37~nt), and a sequence spanned the site when
fITS7 was found with at least 60~nt after its start. SHs are the SH codes of the UNITE records. Script:
\path{stage3/short58_motif_db.py}; results in \path{benchmarks/copy_checks/short58_motif/unite}. These analyses are
exploratory and fix no rule.
