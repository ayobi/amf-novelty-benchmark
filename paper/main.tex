\begin{abstract}
\noindent
1.~Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (AMF) are named from environmental DNA by three systems built on different
ribosomal DNA (rDNA) regions: MaarjAM virtual taxa (VTs) on the small-subunit gene (SSU), UNITE species
hypotheses (SHs) on the internal transcribed spacer (ITS), and species clades on large-subunit (LSU) reference
trees. The names rarely agree, and rDNA copies differ within one AMF genome, so one fungus can look like several
taxa and a divergent copy like a new one. We asked how the names relate, whether they combine into one reliable
call, and how well that call flags absent species.

\noindent
2.~We named \NCopiesRetained{} long-read rDNA copies spanning SSU, ITS and LSU, from \NCultures{} cultures of
\NSpecies{} species, with all three systems. We withheld each culture and species in turn, also removed withheld
species from the LSU reference, tested \LgTestCultures{} cultures from other collections against predictions
fixed in advance, and checked divergent copies on trees and for artifacts and rRNA structure.

\noindent
3.~SHs mostly nest within LSU species, and these within VTs. VTs separated at most \VTResolvedZero{} of
\NSpecies{} species, whereas single cultures spanned 2--\SHSplitMax{} SHs (\SHSplitCultures{} of \SHSplitDenom{}
cultures). Copies with a shortened 5.8S gene resembled new lineages but share one deletion across isolates of several
species: they are genomic paralogs, not artifacts. On trees, species formed clades; cultures rarely did. On the
Kr\"uger fragment, 97\% identity split \LongKrNSSplit{} of copies from their culture, against \LongITSNESplit{} on
the full ITS at 98.5\%. With a
culture withheld, combined names kept the species, alone or in a group, for \KnownKeptITS{} of \KnownN{} copies.
For withheld species also removed from the LSU reference, \MfourFKvOnePct{} of copies were still called known,
\MfourFKvTwoPct{} after an ITS distance rule (\MfourControlPct{} and \MfourControlTwoPct{} with the reference
intact). All remaining errors fell within \NComplexes{} species complexes (\NComplexSpecies{} species) that the
markers cannot separate. In cultures from other collections, the only false-known calls (\LgFK{} of \LgNovelN{}
sequences) were those predicted.

\noindent
4.~Novelty can be called from rDNA only where the markers separate species, and only from evidence independent
of the database that defines what is known. Divergent copies should be recognized before taxa are counted, and
inseparable species reported as groups.

\vspace{0.6em}
\noindent
\textbf{Keywords.} arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi; Glomeromycota; rDNA intragenomic variation; MaarjAM; UNITE;
novelty detection; phylogenetic placement; long-read metabarcoding.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ============================================================================
Surveys of arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (AMF) name what they find in three incompatible ways, so results
from different markers cannot be pooled. All three read the ribosomal DNA (rDNA) unit, which holds the genes
for the small-subunit (SSU, 18S), 5.8S and large-subunit (LSU, 28S) ribosomal RNAs, separated by two internal
transcribed spacers, ITS1 and ITS2 (with the 5.8S gene between them, the ITS; Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}a). SSU
studies report MaarjAM virtual taxa (VTs), groups of SSU sequences defined by phylogeny and at least 97\%
similarity \cite{opik2010maarjam,opik2014virtual}. ITS studies report UNITE species hypotheses (SHs), clusters
of ITS sequences at fixed distance thresholds \cite{koljalg2013unified,nilsson2019unite}. LSU studies place
reads on curated reference trees and name them by the species clade they fall in
\cite{kruger2012phylogenetic,delavaux2021lsu,delavaux2022lsu,delavaux2024lsu}. No agreed table maps one system
onto another, and global occurrence databases such as GlobalAMFungi keep the datasets apart
\cite{vetrovsky2023globalamfungi}. The names in the three databases were assigned independently, many reference
sequences are unidentified environmental clones, and the taxonomy itself keeps moving: the family Glomeraceae
has recently been divided into several families \cite{dasilva2024families}, and a phylogenetic classification
of Glomeromycota has added new orders and families, some described from environmental DNA
\cite{tedersoo2024amf}.

AMF genomes make the mapping harder. Their rDNA copies are not made uniform: copies within one individual
differ \cite{pawlowska2004organization}, a single \spn{Rhizophagus irregularis} genome carries only ten or eleven
complete 45S rDNA units that differ in sequence and are not arranged in tandem \cite{maeda2018rdna}, and copies
within a culture can differ by several percent in the ITS \cite{vankuren2013rdna,stefani2025rdna}. One genome
can therefore look like several taxa in the ITS, and a divergent copy can look like an unknown lineage. The
biology of AMF complicates naming further. A mycelium holds many nuclei, which in dikaryotic strains belong to
two parental nucleotypes whose ratio shifts with the host plant \cite{kokkoris2021host}; nucleotype frequencies
can change quickly after a host shift \cite{angelard2014rapid}; and \spn{Rhizophagus irregularis} forms spores
of two morphotypes, which has confused names in culture collections \cite{kokkoris2024dimorphic}. A
genome-based species classification has recently been proposed for Glomeromycotina \cite{corradi2025reform}.
Any claim of a new AMF taxon therefore has to rule out divergent copies first. Rules for deciding when an ITS
sequence is new have been calibrated for fungi in general \cite{obrien2026flag}, but not for AMF, in which three
naming systems and variation within genomes interact.

Long reads that span the SSU, ITS and LSU link all three markers within a single read. Amplicons of about
2.5~kb were introduced to bring the three barcoding regions into one dataset \cite{kolarikova2021pacbio}, and a
large collection of such copies from identified cultures now exists \cite{stefani2025rdna}. In this collection,
every copy can be named by all three systems at once, and every culture serves as ground truth for its own
copies. We used it to ask four questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item How do VTs, SHs and LSU species clades map onto each other and onto cultured species?
\item Which kinds of divergent copies exist, are they genuine genomic copies, and how does each mislead naming?
\item Can the three names be combined into one reliable call?
\item How often is a species that no reference culture holds called a known species, and how much does the
answer depend on what the external databases contain?
\end{enumerate}
This work is part of amfsplit, a long-read AMF metabarcoding tool built on ITSxRust v0.3.0
\cite{obrien2026itsxrust}. We report the part that names each copy in the three systems and decides when a copy
is new (Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}). Cutting reads into the regions of the three databases is described only as
far as the deposited copies need; the tool's performance on environmental reads, and its correction of
abundances for copy number, will be reported with the tool.

\section{Materials and methods}
% ============================================================================
All analyses read one fixed, versioned reference of culture copies, and every quantity learned from the
cultures was refitted without the withheld cultures in each test (Section~\ref{sec:tests}). Software versions,
parameters and the scripts are given in Supplementary Methods S1--S8 and in the repository (Data and code
availability).

\subsection{Culture copies and barcode regions}\label{sec:data}
We used the \NCopiesIn{} rDNA copies deposited by Stefani et al.\ \cite{stefani2025rdna} (GenBank
PX207096--PX207274 and PX214440--PX215164). Each copy is a PacBio read of about 2.8~kb that spans most of the
SSU, the whole ITS and the first part of the LSU (Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}a). Their study reports PacBio rDNA
for \StefaniCultures{} cultures of \StefaniSpecies{} species and three protein-coding genes for
\StefaniPCCultures{} cultures of \StefaniPCSpecies{} phylogenetic species; we used the rDNA copies and, only to
examine species that rDNA cannot separate, the protein-coding genes (Section~\ref{sec:stefani}). The deposit
carries \DepositLabels{} culture labels and \DepositNames{} names (\DepositSpecies{} species and
\DepositGenusOnly{} named only to genus), the same names as in their culture tables. Three labels hold two
lineages each (mixed cultures), which we counted as separate cultures, giving \NCultures{} cultures. We used the
names as deposited, treating \spn{Rhizoglomus} and \spn{Rhizophagus}, both in use for this lineage, as one genus
where genera are compared; the reorganization of the Glomerales \cite{dasilva2024families} postdates most
deposits, and a name that is outdated in a database is not an error of the marker.

In each copy, ITSxRust located the SSU, ITS1, 5.8S, ITS2 and LSU, and from each copy we cut, at the original
primer sites, the fragment each database was built on: NS31--AM1 for MaarjAM
\cite{simon1992specific,helgason1998ploughing,opik2010maarjam}, the full ITS (ITS1, 5.8S and ITS2) for UNITE,
and LROR--FLR2 for the LSU reference \cite{bunyard1994morchella,trouvelot1999visualization,delavaux2021lsu}.
Four further fragments used in AMF studies were cut the same way (Table~\ref{tab:stage1}; Methods S1). The trees
and the structure check used the three regions that together make up the whole copy: the SSU flank (all SSU
sequence in the copy), the full ITS and the LSU flank (all LSU sequence).

The reference was built from the copies and a file of curation edits, each of which stated in advance what the
data had to look like; a failed expectation stopped the build (Table~\ref{tab:edits}; Methods S2). The final
version (version~4) holds \NCopiesRetained{} copies of \NCultures{} cultures with \RefNames{} names:
\NSpecies{} named species and \RefGenusOnly{} names given only to genus. Within a culture, most copies share one
main rDNA type. Copies whose 5.8S gene differed by more than 3\% from that of the culture's medoid (the copy closest to
all others of its culture) were set apart as \emph{divergent} and split into two classes: \emph{short-5.8S} copies, whose 5.8S is at least 3~bp
shorter than that of the culture's main type, and \emph{same-length} copies. All other copies are
\emph{main-type} copies.

The distance between two sequences was the number of differences (substitutions, insertions and deletions) in
their best overlap, as a percentage of the shorter sequence (edlib \cite{sosic2017edlib}).

\subsection{Naming each copy in the three systems}\label{sec:naming}
Each copy was searched with BLAST+ \cite{camacho2009blast} against the database of each system, with the
identity thresholds of that system (Table~\ref{tab:arms}): its SSU flank against MaarjAM, whose references span
the NS31--AM1 fragment (the gDAT snapshot of July 2020 \cite{opik2010maarjam}); its full ITS against UNITE~10.0
(the full UNITE+INSD set of 19 February 2025 \cite{nilsson2019unite,unite2025release}); and its LROR--FLR2
fragment against the LSU reference of Delavaux et al.\ (V18 of May 2025
\cite{delavaux2021lsu,delavaux2022lsu,delavaux2024lsu}). Hits to any copy of the culture reference were dropped, so no
copy could take its name from itself or from a copy of its own culture. Instead of the best hit alone, each
copy kept every name whose identity lay within a small margin of its best hit (its \emph{candidate names}).
The margin was the smallest at which the main-type copies of at least 99\% of cultures share a name, and names
that split the main-type copies of one culture were merged when the copies on either side lay within 1\% of each
other in the SSU (Table~\ref{tab:sets}; Methods S3). For the LSU,
copies were also placed on the V18 reference tree \cite{barbera2019epang}, and a placement within a clade
that holds a single V18 species was read as that species' clade (the \emph{LSU species clade}; Methods S4).

\subsection{Combining the three names}\label{sec:combining}
Each name was linked to the species and genera of the reference cultures whose copies carry it
(Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}b). In each system, a copy's name was \emph{carried} when at least one reference
culture carries it and \emph{not carried} when none does; a copy without a hit had no name in that system. The
combined call kept the genera shared by all carried names and the species shared by the carried names that are
linked to named species. The result was a species, a species group (several species that the names cannot
split), a genus, \emph{discordant} (the carried names share no genus) or unplaced.

\subsection{Deciding when a copy is new}\label{sec:novelty}
Before a copy was called new, simpler explanations were tested in a fixed order (Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}b;
Methods S5):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Likely artifact.} Against the nearest main-type copy of another culture, the SSU differs by at
least 1\% and by at least half as much as the full ITS. In functional rDNA the SSU changes far more slowly than
the ITS (Section~\ref{sec:trees}), whereas random errors, and copies freed from selection, change both at
similar rates.
\item \textbf{Divergent copy.} The copy's 5.8S is at least 3~bp shorter than, and at least 3\% different from,
the main-type 5.8S of the nearest culture; or its best VT or SH is reached, in at least two other cultures, only
by short-5.8S copies (a \emph{paralog name}). Such copies are named by their VT alone, because their SSU is that
of their genome. A same-length 5.8S that differs by 3\% or more is only flagged.
\item \textbf{Near a VT threshold.} The copy has no VT name, but its best VT lies within one percentage point
of the 97\% threshold.
\item \textbf{New.} A species call was demoted to \emph{new species in a known genus} when the VT or the LSU
name is not carried, when the carried names share a genus but no species, or when the full ITS lies farther
from every reference culture than the within-species spread (the 99th percentile of the ITS distance between
cultures of one species; the \emph{ITS distance rule}). A copy none of whose names is carried was called a
\emph{new lineage}, which has no genus. An SH that is not carried does not demote a call; SHs often split a
single genome (Section~\ref{sec:nest}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Species complexes}\label{sec:complexes}
Some named species cannot be told apart by any of the three markers, and we defined this from the reference
alone. For each marker (full ITS, LROR--FLR2 and SSU flank), the \emph{within-species spread} is the 99th
percentile of the distance from a main-type copy to the nearest main-type copy of another culture of the same
species: \ITSSpread{} in the ITS, \LSUSpread{} in the LSU and \SSUSpread{} in the SSU. Two species are
\emph{inseparable} when, on every marker, their nearest copies lie no further apart than this spread, so that a
copy of one falls inside the spread of the other. Inseparable pairs that share a species were joined into a
\emph{complex}, and a complex is reported as one unit, a species group, wherever a species call is made. The
rule uses no test result and adds no threshold beyond the percentile that the ITS distance rule already uses.
Because that percentile was chosen after the distances in these cultures had been seen, the ITS distance rule
and the complexes were also recomputed together at the 95th and 97.5th percentiles (Methods S6).

\subsection{Tests with withheld cultures and species}\label{sec:tests}
We tested the calls by withholding parts of the reference and asking how the withheld copies were named. In
each test round, everything learned from the cultures (merged names, margins, the links from names to species,
the comparisons of the divergent-copy test, the paralog names and the limit of the ITS distance rule) was
refitted on the remaining cultures only (Methods S6).

\emph{Withheld cultures.} In 148 rounds, one culture label was withheld, together with both lineages where a
label holds a mixed culture. The question was whether the main-type copies of species still held by another culture kept a
call that contains their species.

\emph{Withheld species.} In 50 rounds, one named species was withheld with all its cultures (\NSpecies{}
rounds), or one culture not named to species (13 rounds). A \emph{false-known call} is a call of a known species
or species group for a copy whose species has no culture left in the reference.

\emph{Withheld from the LSU reference as well.} In the tests above the three databases stayed intact, so a
withheld species could still have its own VT, SH or LSU clade. In the \emph{LSU-removal test}, each withheld
species was also removed from the LSU reference: the reference alignment was rebuilt without its records, the
model refitted and all copies placed again. MaarjAM and UNITE were not edited, so a withheld species kept any VT
or SH it has there; this matters because a VT that is not carried demotes a call and an SH does not. The ITS
distance rule was added after two species, \spn{Glomus chinense} and \spn{Glomus rugosae}, had been run in this
test, and these are reported as development species. The \MfourFolds{} other species whose LSU records could be
removed (the \emph{test species}) were run after the rule had been fixed; \spn{\MfourAbsentTarget}, which the LSU
reference does not hold by name, is reported apart. In \GenusFolds{} further rounds, every culture of a genus and
the genus's LSU records were withheld together.

These cultures were used to develop the methods, so every result from them is a development estimate. The
independent test is described next.

\subsection{Cultures from other collections}\label{sec:lgdb-methods}
LongGloDB v1 (July 2026) \cite{glodb} holds \LgTotal{} curated SSU--ITS--LSU amplicon sequence variants (ASVs)
from AMF culture collections. We left out the \LgStefani{} ASVs that carry the laboratory codes of the Stefani et
al.\ cultures (STFP and STFI): \LgStefaniIdentical{} of them are identical in full ITS to a copy in our
reference, so they are the same cultures processed again. A further \LgCodeShared{} cultures share a culture code
with a Stefani label (\LgCodeConflicts{} of them under another species name) and were left out too. The test set
holds the remaining \LgTestASVs{} ASVs of \LgTestCultures{} cultures from three other laboratories:
\LgKnownN{} ASVs of \LgKnownCultures{} cultures of species the reference holds, \LgNovelSpN{} of species it
does not hold in genera it does, \LgNovelLinN{} of genera it does not hold, and \LgGenusOnlyN{} named only to
genus. The ASVs of absent species come from \LgNovelCultures{} cultures of \LgNovelSpecies{} species.

Before any test sequence was searched, placed or scored, we applied the complex rule
(Section~\ref{sec:complexes}) to the test ASVs and fixed its predictions, together with a protocol and two
amendments that set the outcomes and analyses, by recording their checksums, digital fingerprints that show any
later change (Methods S7). A test ASV can be
wrongly called a known species only if it lies inside the within-species spread of that species on all three
markers, so a false-known call outside the predicted set would contradict the result of
Section~\ref{sec:errorcomplexes}. The rules were used unchanged and learned only from the \NCultures{}
reference cultures, so no test sequence influenced them. The test sequences were cut like the deposited copies
and named against the same database releases, first with the LSU reference intact and then with each of the
\LgFolds{} absent species removed from it in its own round, as in the LSU-removal test.

The test amplicons begin about 60~bp inside the NS31--AM1 fragment, so they rarely cover the 95\% of a MaarjAM
reference that the VT search requires, and only \LgVTAnchor{} of the \LgTestASVs{} ASVs had a VT hit that
passed. The main analysis applied the rules as fixed. A second analysis, also fixed before the run, counted VT
coverage over the part of each MaarjAM reference that the ASV spans and changed nothing else.

\subsection{Trees, artifact check and structure check}\label{sec:checkmethods}
These checks were fixed in a protocol, and its checksum recorded, before they were run; the structure check was
added in a dated addendum that was likewise fixed before its results were seen (Methods S8).

\emph{Trees.} For each order, identical sequences were collapsed and the copies aligned with MAFFT L-INS-i
\cite{katoh2013mafft}, separately for the SSU flank, the full ITS and the LSU flank, and for the three joined
end to end (the \emph{linked copy}). Maximum-likelihood trees were inferred with IQ-TREE~2
\cite{minh2020iqtree} with ModelFinder \cite{kalyaanamoorthy2017modelfinder}, 1,000 ultrafast bootstraps
\cite{hoang2018ufboot} and 1,000 SH-aLRT replicates \cite{guindon2010phyml}. A culture, named species or complex
was \emph{monophyletic} when one branch of the unrooted tree separated exactly its copies from all others, and
\emph{strongly non-monophyletic} when a branch that contradicts it had SH-aLRT $\ge80$ and ultrafast bootstrap
$\ge95$. Each group was tested with all copies and with the divergent copies removed.

\emph{Artifact check.} Each divergent copy was aligned to the most similar main-type copy of its own culture. A
deletion was \emph{long} when it reached, in its region, the 99th percentile of the longest deletion between
main-type copies of one culture, with a floor of 3~nt: \ArtLConserved{}~nt in the SSU, 5.8S and LSU and
\ArtLSpacer{}~nt in ITS1 and ITS2. A divergent copy \emph{recurred} when a copy within 1.0\% of it in full ITS
occurred under another culture label, that is, in another isolate. The verdicts were fixed in advance: a
recurring copy without long deletions is a paralog; a recurring copy with them is a recurrent deleted copy
(genomic, possibly a pseudogene); a single copy with them is a probable artifact; and a single copy without them
is unresolved.

\emph{Structure check.} To ask whether divergent copies keep the structure of functional rRNA, the SSU, 5.8S
and LSU of every copy were aligned with Infernal \cite{nawrocki2013infernal} to the Rfam models of eukaryotic SSU
rRNA (RF01960), 5.8S rRNA (RF00002) and eukaryotic LSU rRNA (RF02543) \cite{ontiverospalacios2025rfam}, which
describe both the sequence and the secondary structure of each RNA family. Each base pair of the consensus
structure that a copy covers was \emph{broken} when it was not a Watson--Crick or G$\cdot$U pair, or when one
side was deleted. A copy was \emph{degraded} in a region when its share of broken pairs exceeded the 99th
percentile among main-type copies, and \emph{irregular} when its substitutions or insertion and deletion
events, counted against the most similar main-type copy of its culture, exceeded the 99th percentile among
main-type copies. The verdict for the short-5.8S class was fixed in advance: \emph{structurally intact} if at
most 10\% of its copies were degraded in each region and its shared deletion removed only unpaired positions of
the consensus structure; \emph{structurally degraded}, as expected of pseudogenes, if at least 50\% were
degraded in some region or the shared deletion removed paired positions; and \emph{mixed} otherwise. The ITS2 of
every copy was folded with RNAfold \cite{lorenz2011viennarna}, for description only.

\subsection{Distances at each level of relatedness}\label{sec:distmethods}
To explore similarity thresholds without fixing any rule, we measured, for every main-type copy, the distance to
its nearest main-type copy in four classes: the same culture, another culture of the same species, another
species of the same genus, and another genus of the same order. We did this for the NS31--AM1 fragment, on
which VTs are defined, the whole SSU flank, the full ITS and LROR--FLR2, and we also measured the distances
between the medoid copies of every pair of cultures within an order. The same distances were measured on two
longer regions, the Kr\"uger fragment and the whole copy. We counted what the short type does to clusters and to
the tree of each marker, and tested a sequence motif that flags it (Methods S8).

\section{Results}
% ============================================================================
\subsection{The culture copies}\label{sec:copies}
ITSxRust located the regions in all \NCopiesIn{} deposited copies, and every fragment whose primers lie inside a
copy was recovered (Table~\ref{tab:stage1}). The fragments are close in length to the amplicons behind the databases:
NS31--AM1 has a median length of 508~bp without its primers and about 550~bp with them, the size of the
fragment behind MaarjAM virtual taxa. The 28 copies without an NS31--AM1 fragment are exactly the
Paraglomerales copies, in which the AM1 primer does not bind.

Curation changed little (Table~\ref{tab:edits}). One copy was dropped because its SSU and ITS differ from the
other copies of its culture at the same rate, the signature of an error or a pseudogene. One culture held a

\subsection{The three naming systems nest}\label{sec:nest}
The SSU gave a VT name to \VTAssigned{} of the \NCopiesRetained{} copies. Counting each culture by its most
common VT, \VTSpeciesMulti{} of \VTSpeciesN{} named species span more than one VT, and \VTUnitsMulti{} of
\VTUnitsN{} VTs hold more than one species; VTX00199 alone holds four \spn{Glomus} species. The nearest LSU
reference gives a culture its own species for \LSUOwnSpecies{} of the \LSUOwnDenom{} named cultures whose species
V18 holds (\LSUOwnPct{}; \LSUCongener{} get a congener and \LSUNoCall{} no name), and the right genus for
\LSUGenusRight{} that get a name. V18 itself, however, holds two records for nearly every species (460 records
under 234 species labels: 215 labels with two records, five with three or four, and 14 with one); 45 of the 220
labels with two or more records hold two records that are less than 97\% identical, and when each record was
left out in turn, only 72\% of records returned their own species.

UNITE+INSD, which holds all public sequences, named \SHAssignedFull{} copies (\SHAssignedFullPct{}), against
\SHAssignedGen{} (\SHAssignedGenPct{}) for the general release, which keeps one representative per SH. An SH is
finer than a genome: the main-type copies of \SHSplitCultures{} of the \SHSplitDenom{} cultures with two or more
SH names fall in 2--\SHSplitMax{} SHs. Counting each culture by its most common SH, \SHUnitsMulti{} of
\SHUnitsN{} SHs hold two named species, and VTs split \SHSplitByVT{} of \SHSplitByVTN{} SHs; once SHs are merged
into one group per genome, one group is shared by \spn{Glomus macrocarpum} and \spn{Glomus rugosae}. The LSU
splits \SHSplitByLSU{} of \SHSplitByLSUN{} SHs (into the V18 species \spn{Diversispora varaderana} and
\spn{Diversispora bareai}), and VTs split \LSUSplitByVT{} of \LSUSplitByVTN{} LSU names. So SHs mostly sit inside
LSU species, which mostly sit inside VTs.

Within a culture the SSU hardly varies (median 0.07\%, 95th percentile 0.54\%, 99th percentile 0.95\%). Merging two
pairs of VTs, VTX00113 with VTX00115 and VTX00125 with VTX00222, gives every culture one consistent set of VT
names (Table~\ref{tab:sets}), and the margin needed to absorb the SSU differences within cultures (\VTDelta{}
points) costs three species of resolution. SHs merged into one group per genome separate \SHResolved{} of the
\NSpecies{} species, against \VTResolved{} for VTs; this is an upper bound, because the merges were learned on the
same cultures.

\subsection{Divergent copies are genomic paralogs}\label{sec:divergent}
The two divergent classes mislead each marker differently (Table~\ref{tab:divergent}). Short-5.8S copies are
well represented in public data, as expected for the variable, non-tandem rDNA units of a single genome
\cite{maeda2018rdna}: their median best SH hit (\DivSHMedian{}) is closer than that of main-type copies
(\MainSHMedian{}). Divergent copies reach \DivSHs{} SHs, of which \ParalogSH{} are reached by no main-type copy
(paralog SHs, \ParalogPlaceholder{} of them under placeholder names) and \DivSHOneMain{} by one main-type copy
each (Table~\ref{tab:paralog}). The short copies of \spn{Septoglomus viscosum} are the only culture copies that
reach VTX00409, about two points clear of VTX00063. In \spn{Gigaspora rosea}, four cultures carry the same three
or four rDNA types, identical across cultures and up to 6.2\% apart in the LSU, each in its own SH named
\spn{Gigaspora rosea}. These types are not confined to one isolate held in several collections: in LongGloDB
(Section~\ref{sec:lgdb}), INVAM cultures under other accession codes (\LgRoseaCultures{}; FL105 is INVAM's
reference culture for the species) carry ASVs within \LgRoseaMaxITS{} of them in full ITS.

\paragraph{They are not artifacts.} Under the rule fixed in advance (Table~\ref{tab:artefacts}),
\ArtSameParalog{} same-length copies are paralogs (the \spn{Gigaspora rosea} types, which match main-type copies
of other cultures), \ArtShortRecDel{} short-5.8S copies are recurrent deleted copies, and \ArtShortArtefact{}
short-5.8S and \ArtSameArtefact{} same-length copies are probable artifacts. The rule counts a type as recurring
only when its whole ITS recurs, and many short-5.8S copies fail that test because their spacers differ. Their
deletion does not. In a reading made after the check, every one of the \NShort{} short-5.8S copies, the
\ShDelRecurArtefact{} probable artifacts included, carries a 5.8S deletion of the same length, starting within
2~nt, as a copy under another culture label. In \spn{Rhizophagus} and \spn{[Rhizoglomus]}, all \ShSixCopies{}
short copies share one 6-nt deletion, found in \ShSixCultures{} cultures of five named species and
\spn{Rhizophagus} sp., and so does one copy filed as main type (PX214776.1, \spn{Rhizophagus irregularis}
4569\_1185B, the main-type copy in SH1337353; Table~\ref{tab:paralog}). A deletion shared across species and
isolates is not a PCR or sequencing error, so the short-5.8S copies form a genomic paralog family. The
same-length probable artifacts rest on ITS2 deletions of 8--9~nt; with long deletions read as at least 10~nt,
their number falls to \ArtSameArtefactTen{}.

\paragraph{Their 5.8S has lost part of one hairpin.} The SSU of short-5.8S copies kept its secondary structure:
\FnSSUDeg{} of the \FnShortN{} copies were degraded, and their share of broken pairs did not differ from that of
the main-type copies of their culture (median \FnSSUBrokenShort{} against \FnSSUBrokenBase{} among main-type
copies; paired Wilcoxon test, $P=\FnSSUP{}$). Their LSU carries far more substitutions than main-type copies
(median \FnLSUSubs{} against \FnLSUSubsBase{} per 100 aligned positions), but most of these keep the base
pairing: \FnLSUDeg{} copies (\FnLSUDegPct{}) were degraded, and the share of broken pairs differed little
(\FnLSUBrokenShort{} against \FnLSUBrokenBase{}; $P=\FnLSUP{}$). The 5.8S differs most: \FnFiveIrr{} of the
\FnShortN{} copies were irregular, and broken pairs rose from \FnFiveBrokenBase{} to \FnFiveBrokenShort{} (median
paired difference \FnFiveDiff{} points; $P$~\FnFiveP{}). In all \FnHairpinShort{} short copies of
\spn{Rhizophagus} and \spn{[Rhizoglomus]}, the deletion removes three of the nine base pairs of a hairpin near
the 3$'$ end of the 5.8S (consensus positions 121--130 of the Rfam model), whereas in the \FnUnpairedShort{}
\spn{Septoglomus viscosum} copies it removes only unpaired positions. Under the rule fixed in advance, the
short-5.8S class is therefore \FnVerdictShort{}, as pseudogenes would be. The hairpin is shortened rather than
destroyed, however. The remaining six-pair stem is made only of Watson--Crick pairs in \FnStemAllWC{} of the
\FnHairpinShort{} copies, and its new loop is UUCG in \FnLoopUUCG{}, UUUG in \FnLoopUUUG{} and CUCG in
\FnLoopCUCG{}; a UUCG loop closed by a C--G pair, as in most of these copies, forms an unusually stable
hairpin \cite{tuerk1988cuucgg}. The main-type copies with the same shortened hairpin, PX214776.1 and PX214775.1
(\spn{Glomus} sp.\ 5089\_2351A), were among those flagged by the artifact check. The same-length copies were
\FnVerdictSame{}: none was degraded in any region. In ITS2, the predicted folds of short copies differed from
those of their culture's main type more than main-type copies differ from each other (median base-pair distance
\FnITSTwoBPShort{} against \FnITSTwoBPBase{} per nucleotide), with similar folding energies ($\FnITSTwoMFEShort{}$
against $\FnITSTwoMFEBase{}$ kcal\,mol$^{-1}$ per nucleotide).

\subsection{On trees, species form clades but cultures do not}\label{sec:trees}
Cultures were rarely monophyletic: \MonoCultPctMin{}--\MonoCultPctMax{} of the \MonoCultN{} cultures with two or
more copies, depending on the region and on whether divergent copies were kept (Table~\ref{tab:monophyly}). A
culture that is not monophyletic typically has most of its copies outside any clade made only of its own copies
(median \MonoCultEscMedian{} on the linked copy, main type), mixed with copies of other cultures of the same
species (Figure~\ref{fig:tree}), as expected if the rDNA variants of a genome are older than the isolates that
carry them. Named species mostly are clades: \MonoSpSSUAll{}, \MonoSpITSAll{} and \MonoSpLSUAll{} of the
\NSpecies{} species on the SSU flank, full ITS and LSU flank, and \MonoSpLinkedAll{} on the linked copy. Removing
the divergent copies made \MonoFixedN{} more species monophyletic on the linked copy (\spn{Rhizophagus
intraradices}, \spn{Rhizophagus irregularis}, \spn{Rhizophagus prolifer} and \spn{[Rhizoglomus]
vesiculiferum}), \MonoSpLinkedMain{} of \NSpecies{} in all. The two that remain, \spn{Diversispora aestuarii} and
\spn{Glomus rugosae} (only the latter strongly), belong to species complexes, and all four complexes are
monophyletic as units (\MonoComplexLinkedMain{}).

Every one of the \FKInsideVTwo{} false-known calls left after the ITS rule in the test species is a call to a
member of the species' own complex. Without the ITS rule, \FKInsideVOne{} of the \MfourFKvOne{} errors are
inside a complex and \FKOutsideVOne{} (\FKOutsideVOnePct{} of the withheld copies) are not; with the LSU reference intact,
\FKOutsideCtlOne{} and \FKOutsideCtlTwo{} copies fall outside a complex without and with the rule. The ITS rule
thus removes the errors that the markers could in principle have avoided, and what is left is the limit of the
markers themselves. The complexes were derived from all cultures, including the withheld species, so this shows
that the errors are confined to pairs that the reference already shows to be inseparable. It does not show that
an undescribed relative of a complex member would be caught, which, by definition, it would not.

\section{Discussion}
% ============================================================================
Linked copies turn the correspondence between VTs, SHs and LSU species from a guess into a measurement, and
they show where each naming system misleads.

\subsection{The three systems nest rather than match}
SHs mostly sit inside LSU species, which mostly sit inside VTs. VT counts therefore underestimate species (at
best, VTs separate \VTResolvedZero{} of \NSpecies{}), and SH counts overestimate them, because one genome spans up
to \SHSplitMax{} SHs. Surveys that count SHs as taxa inflate AMF richness, and part of that inflation comes from
variation within genomes. Grouping SHs by genome restores most of the species signal in our cultures, but the
groups were learned from these same cultures, and whether they transfer to field sequences is untested. The
2.5-kb amplicons were introduced to bring the three regions into one dataset \cite{kolarikova2021pacbio}; what
was missing is a measured correspondence between the names, which linked copies supply.

\subsection{Divergent copies are genomic; whether they work is open}
Variation among the rDNA copies of an AMF genome has long been known
\cite{pawlowska2004organization,vankuren2013rdna}, yet it has been largely overlooked in ecological and
taxonomic work \cite{stefani2025rdna}, and a genome-based species classification has only recently been proposed
\cite{corradi2025reform}. What linked copies add is a measure of how this variation misleads each marker.
Short-5.8S copies look like new lineages in the LSU and form their own SHs and VTs, several under placeholder
names in UNITE. The short-5.8S test recognizes most of them from their sequence alone, but less often when the
whole species is unknown, because no related culture with a 5.8S of the right length is then available for
comparison. They are not artifacts: the same 5.8S deletion recurs across isolates and species, and on trees the
short copies of different species group together. A long deletion that recurs should therefore be read as part
of a known genome, not discarded as a singleton.

Being part of a genome does not make the short copies harmless. A species that carries them has two structural
variants of the marker, so the short copies form ITS clusters of their own (\ParITSNEShort{} at 98.5\%,
Table~\ref{tab:clusters}), and the ITS and LSU tell a different history from the SSU: on the LSU, the short copies
of \spn{Rhizophagus intraradices} and \spn{Rhizophagus prolifer} sit next to \spn{Sclerocystis} rather than with
their own species. Because the class has a fixed signature, it can be flagged before clustering or tree
building: in \spn{Rhizophagus}, a 13-nt motif in the 5.8S marks every short copy and no other. In UNITE the same
motif sorts \spn{Rhizophagus} SHs almost perfectly, and about one in five is made only of the short type, so the
paralog inflates SH counts in the public database as it does in the cultures. Such a flag has to be read with the
genus, because the motif is also the normal 5.8S of \spn{Oehlia diaphana} and is carried by hundreds of unplaced
Glomeraceae sequences, which only placement can sort out; and it needs the 5.8S, which an ITS2 trimmed by ITSx no
longer has. Species are then best counted from main-type copies, and the short type analyzed as a locus of its
own rather than mixed into a species tree.

Whether the short copies work is a separate question. Their SSU keeps its base pairing, and their LSU, although
rich in substitutions, mostly keeps it too. The deletion shortens one hairpin of the 5.8S, so under the rule we
fixed in advance the class counts as structurally degraded, as pseudogenes would be; yet the shortened stem is
made of Watson--Crick pairs and is mostly closed by a UUCG loop, a combination known for stable hairpins
\cite{tuerk1988cuucgg}. DNA alone cannot tell a transcribed and processed copy from a silent one. Sequencing the
rRNA or its precursors from the same cultures would show whether the short type ends up in ribosomes.
Same-length divergent copies are more ambiguous: co-dominant types of one genome in \spn{Gigaspora}, second
lineages in \spn{Septoglomus}. They should be flagged, not explained away.

\subsection{No identity threshold delivers species}
The distances at each level of relatedness show why the three systems disagree (Figure~\ref{fig:thrnearest}).
The thresholds of the three databases fall at different points on the scale of AMF variation. On the NS31--AM1
fragment, 97\% identity would join almost every copy with a congener (\ThrVTCongenerDB{}) and some with another
genus, mostly in \spn{Cetraspora}, \spn{Gigaspora} and \spn{Dentiscutata}, which fits the coarse resolution of
VTs (at most \VTResolvedZero{} of \NSpecies{} species). On the full ITS, the 98.5\% of SHs lies within the
variation of a single culture, so one genome often spans several SHs. On the LSU, the 97\% of
V18 matches the spread within species, but \ThrLSUCongener{} of copies have a congener inside it. On no region
does one threshold separate species, in line with the absence of a barcode gap that Stefani et al.\ reported for
each region \cite{stefani2025rdna}, so moving a threshold only trades lumping against splitting. Taken together,
the regions do better: on the Kr\"uger fragment, 97\% splits few genomes and, outside the complexes, joins almost
no species, and the whole copy does the same at a higher identity. What no cut-off removes are the complexes and
the short paralog. Combined names,
with complexes reported as groups, avoid that trade-off where the markers allow it. The SSU, by contrast, behaves
predictably: between cultures its distance was a median of \ThrPairsRatio{} times the ITS distance, and no pair of
cultures met the artifact test, so the test is conservative: an SSU that differs by at least half as much as the
ITS points to an artifact, not to a genuine relative.

\subsection{Novelty has a floor set by the markers}
SSU, ITS and LSU together cannot separate the members of \NComplexes{} complexes, \NComplexSpecies{} species in
all: their nearest copies lie inside each other's within-species spread on every marker. All errors that remain
after the ITS rule fall inside them at the 99th percentile used throughout. The claim holds at every percentile
we tried except for one pair: with the rule and the complexes both at the 95th or 97.5th percentile,
\spn{Rhizophagus irregularis} and \spn{[Rhizoglomus] vesiculiferum} are no longer a complex, and the
\spn{Rhizophagus irregularis} copies called \spn{[Rhizoglomus] vesiculiferum} become errors outside one
(Table~\ref{tab:percentile}). Whether these two names can be separated on rDNA is therefore the open question,
not whether the floor exists. Within a complex, a species call is honest only as a group, and a call of novelty
is impossible: an undescribed relative inside the spread of a known species cannot be told from that species on
these regions. The alternative reading is that some of these names are not separate lineages, a question for
taxonomists; \spn{Diversispora aestuarii} and \spn{Diversispora varaderana} differ by 0.0\% in the SSU and 1.1\%
in the LSU. The rule is driven by the data and will change as cultures are added: one further pair
(\NearMissPair{}) misses the \NearMissMarker{} limit by \NearMissPoints{} points.

\subsection{Novelty depends on what the reference already knows}
With only the cultures withheld, LSU placement looks like a strong detector of new species, but much of that
strength comes from the withheld species still being in the LSU reference: its clade holds no culture, so its
copies are flagged. Phylogenetic placement was proposed because matching against a database cannot place
sequences of taxa that are absent from it \cite{delavaux2021lsu,delavaux2022lsu}. That holds at the level of
clades, but the LSU-removal test shows its limit at the level of species: with the species removed, a copy is
placed on its nearest named relative and then named after it, and with a genus removed, on the sister genus
(\GenusWrongN{} \spn{Rhizophagus} copies called \spn{Sclerocystis}). An undescribed species is absent from every
reference. Removing it from the LSU reference is therefore closer to the real situation than leaving it in, but
not equal to it: MaarjAM and UNITE were left intact, and many VTs are built from environmental sequences, so a
species that is undescribed yet already sequenced in the field can still have a VT of its own. The VT accounted
for the novelty of \MfourNOne{} of the \MfourHeldN{} copies, so for a species never sequenced the false-known rate
may be higher than reported, by up to about \MfourNOnePct{} of copies. The ITS distance rule does not depend on
external databases and closes part of the gap, but not where the ITS itself overlaps. Pooled over the test
species, false-known calls rose from \MfourControlPct{} with the LSU reference intact to \MfourFKvOnePct{} with
the species removed from it, and fell to \MfourFKvTwoPct{} with the ITS rule.

\subsection{Relation to earlier work on the same cultures}\label{sec:stefani}
Stefani et al.\ \cite{stefani2025rdna} sequenced the copies used here (\StefaniCultures{} cultures of
\StefaniSpecies{} species) and compared rDNA with three protein-coding genes (glomalin, H$^+$-ATPase and RPB1),
sequenced for \StefaniPCCultures{} cultures of \StefaniPCSpecies{} species. They found no barcode gap in any of
the regions, so that no single distance threshold identifies AMF, and concluded that rDNA remains the practical
choice because its primers work across all lineages. Our results agree where they overlap: the complexes are
pairs whose nearest copies lie inside each other's spread on every rDNA region. Two of their observations follow
from the short paralog. The rDNA of \spn{Rhizophagus vesiculifer} (our \spn{[Rhizoglomus] vesiculiferum}) and
\spn{Rhizophagus prolifer} was
paraphyletic in their trees, nested in \spn{Rhizophagus irregularis} and \spn{Rhizophagus intraradices}; on our
linked tree, these four species become clades once the divergent copies are set aside
(Section~\ref{sec:trees}). And their largest within-species distances on the Kr\"uger fragment were in the species
that carry the short type (\spn{R.\ irregularis} 7.7\%, \spn{R.\ vesiculifer} 10.2\%, \spn{R.\ prolifer} and
\spn{R.\ intraradices} up to 11\%) and in \spn{Glomus chinense} and \spn{Glomus rugosae} (7.4\% and 8\%), members
of one of our complexes. Their distances are maximum pairwise distances over all copies, with every gap counted,
and ours are distances to the nearest copy, so the numbers are not directly comparable. The questions differ. They asked
whether a distance threshold identifies a species within one marker; we asked how the three naming systems relate
and how far novelty can be called once divergent copies are accounted for. Their protein-coding sequences are
public (glomalin PV808915--PV809055, RPB1 PV809056--PV809196, H$^+$-ATPase PV809197--PV809333) and could settle
the complexes: if those genes separate \spn{Diversispora aestuarii} from \spn{Diversispora varaderana}, the
complex is a limit of rDNA, and if not, it is a question of naming. We compared them (Table~\ref{tab:pcgenes}). At
the thresholds Stefani et al.\ proposed, the three genes separate almost every pair of congeners that rDNA
separates (\PcBaseline{}; \PcBaselinePct{}). Of the \PcComplexPairsAll{} pairs of species within complexes,
\PcComplexPairsN{} have protein-coding records for both species. Of these, \PcComplexAboveN{} exceed the threshold
on one gene each (\PcComplexAbove{}), but \PcComplexCleanText{} of the \PcComplexComparisons{} comparisons exceeds
the spread within the species themselves. The complexes are therefore not a limit of rDNA alone: on these genes,
too, their members lie as close as cultures of one species, and whether they are separate species is a question
for taxonomy rather than for the choice of marker. Joining the records through culture names also showed that the
RPB1 and H$^+$-ATPase deposits rotate the species names of three cultures: \PcConflictDetail{}. Each of these
records lies \PcConflictNearest{} from records of its culture's species, so the sequences are right and the names
are not; we left the \PcConflicts{} records out.

\subsection{Why the rules are not calibrated per species}
A calibrated rule for calling ITS sequences new exists for fungi in general \cite{obrien2026flag}. Here the limit
of the ITS distance rule is itself a calibrated quantile, the 99th percentile of the distance between cultures of
one species, and it behaves as set: it demoted \ITSDemotedKnown{} of the \KnownN{} copies of known species
(\ITSDemotedKnownPct{}). The 99th percentile was fixed before the test species were run. The 95th would have cut
false-known calls further (\PctNinetyFiveFKPct{} against \PctNinetyNineFKPct{} with the species removed from the
LSU reference) at the cost of demoting \PctNinetyFiveKnownDemotedPct{} of the copies of known species instead of
\PctNinetyNineKnownDemotedPct{} (Table~\ref{tab:percentile}), a trade-off a user can set. The other rules, such as
the demotion when a VT or LSU name is not carried, are not calibrated, because the reference holds too few
cultures per species to fit a threshold for each, and because the traps peculiar to AMF, divergent copies and
species complexes, have to be handled before a calibration can mean anything.

\subsection{Independent cultures}
On \LgTestCultures{} cultures from three other laboratories, with the predictions fixed before the run, the rules
called a species absent from the reference a known species only where the complex rule said they would:
\spn{Gigaspora gigantea} KS302, inside \spn{Gigaspora rosea} on every marker. The test is easier than ours in one
respect and harder in two. Its absent species are far from those of the reference, so it says little about the
sister-species case, which occurred once. Its amplicons start inside the VT fragment, which leaves the VT search
silent unless coverage is counted over the part a sequence spans, and a genus taken from the VT alone was then
wrong when the true genus was absent from the reference (\spn{Racocetra} given \spn{Cetraspora}). And its cultures
come from other collections, some of which lie further from ours in the ITS than our cultures of one species lie
from each other: the ITS distance rule, fitted at the 99th percentile on one collection, demoted \LgNSixDemoted{}
of the \LgKnownN{} ASVs of known species and prevented no false-known call. Its limit should be fitted on more
than one collection before the rule is used on field data.

\subsection{Implications for surveys}
Name at the level the markers support: report VT sets, SHs grouped by genome and LSU species clades, with species
groups where the markers cannot decide, and do not expect any identity threshold to deliver species on its own.
Screen for the short-5.8S class before counting taxa or building trees, and treat paralog SHs and VTs, such as SH1337353 and
VTX00409, as known genomes. Base claims of novelty on evidence that does not come from the database that defines
`known', and read names against a stated taxonomy, because outdated names in a database look like errors of the
marker \cite{dasilva2024families}. Replication across independent libraries is planned for environmental data.

\subsection{Limitations}
Apart from the LongGloDB test, all results come from one collection of cultured species, and these cultures were
used to develop the methods, so the results are development estimates. The LongGloDB test rests on
\LgNovelCultures{} cultures of absent species, covers only distant novelty, and used amplicons on which the VT
search as fixed is silent. The 99th percentile behind the ITS rule and the complexes was chosen after the
distances in these cultures had been seen (Table~\ref{tab:percentile} shows the effect of other choices), and the
complexes were derived from all cultures, including the withheld species. Culture names are treated as truth,
although some are doubtful: \spn{Microdominikia litorea} 5274, some \spn{Gigaspora rosea} cultures, and
\spn{Septoglomus} sp.\ C4 and 5070\_5024A, whose LSU matches \spn{Microviscospora peruviscosa}. Recurrence across
culture labels can overstate independence, because one isolate may be held by several collections under
different codes. MaarjAM and UNITE stay intact in every test; the LSU-removal test removes species from the LSU
reference only. About a quarter of the V18 species labels with several records (51 in the placement audit) do not
form exclusive clades on its backbone. The structure check reads DNA: a copy that keeps its base pairing need not
be transcribed or end up in ribosomes, and a copy that loses a pair need not be silent. The distances at each
level of relatedness are exploratory and fix no rule. Replication on environmental libraries, and the
copy-number model of amfsplit, are outstanding.

\section{Conclusions}
% ============================================================================
A call of novelty for an AMF sequence is only as good as the external references are complete, and it cannot be
made below the resolution of the markers: with the withheld species removed from the LSU reference, some
false-known calls remain, and every one of them lies in a species complex that SSU, ITS and LSU cannot separate.
In cultures from other collections, the only species wrongly called known was the one the complex rule predicted.
Linked long-read copies give an empirical bridge between the three naming systems and show where each one
misleads. Divergent copies, which are genomic paralogs shared across species, must be recognized before taxa are
counted, and species that the markers cannot separate, which protein-coding genes do not separate either, should
be reported as groups.

\subsection*{Methods S1. Barcode fragments}
ITSxRust anchored each read with one nhmmer pass (HMMER~3 \cite{eddy2011hmmer}, ITSx fungal profile
\cite{bengtsson2013itsx}) and delimited the SSU flank, ITS1, 5.8S, ITS2, full ITS and LSU flank. We used tolerant
anchoring thresholds (E-value $\le10^{-3}$, anchor score $\ge15$), because anchoring only delimits regions. A
panel of seven fragments (Table~\ref{tab:stage1}) was cut at the original primer sites, so that each fragment
covers the region its database was built on. The panel used the primers of the SSU virtual taxa (NS31, AM1 and
AML2 \cite{simon1992specific,helgason1998ploughing,lee2008improved,opik2010maarjam}), the SSU V4 primers WANDA and
AMV4.5NF/AMDGR \cite{dumbrell2011distinct,sato2005}, the LSU primers LROR \cite{bunyard1994morchella,delavaux2021lsu}
and FLR2 \cite{trouvelot1999visualization}, the D2 primers FLR3 and FLR4 \cite{gollotte2004diversity}, and the
outer pair AML1 and LSUmBr \cite{lee2008improved,kruger2009dna} of the long amplicon \cite{kolarikova2021pacbio},
LSUmBr as the degenerate consensus of its five variants \cite{schlaeppi2016profiling}. Primers were located with
an IUPAC-aware infix alignment (edlib \cite{sosic2017edlib}) that tolerated edits up to 15\% of the primer length;
fragments were primer-trimmed, and coordinates are 1-based, inclusive and in rRNA-sense orientation. Every primer
sequence was checked against published sources (\path{analysis/primer_check.tsv}). A primer was searched for only
in the part of the read where it can lie (SSU primers before the ITS, LSU primers after it, plus 50~nt). A fragment
is \emph{open} when a primer site lies beyond the end of the copy, and \emph{missing} when the primer is absent
although the copy covers its expected position. Copies were also screened for repeats (concatemers) with the
screen of amfsplit version~0.3.1, which will be described with the tool. No fragment crosses an ITSxRust anchor,
and no copy was flagged as a repeat.

\subsection*{Methods S2. Culture reference}
The names of the deposit are those of the culture tables of Stefani et al.\ (their Tables S2 and S3). Their
\StefaniPCSpecies{} protein-coding species are phylogenetic species, which split \spn{Septoglomus} sp.\ into
three; their \StefaniSpecies{} rDNA species are not itemized, and we used the names of the deposit. A culture is
an organism name plus a culture label. The reference was built by \path{freeze_ref.py} from the fragment tables
and an edits file. Every edit stated first what the data had to look like (organism, label, copy count, VT, copy
class, 5.8S length), and a failed expectation stopped the build before anything was written. Each version
recorded input and output hashes and a fingerprint. The edits leading to version~4 (fingerprint
\RefFingerprint) are listed in Table~\ref{tab:edits}. Copy classes follow the 5.8S: copies whose 5.8S lies more
than 3\% from that of the culture's medoid copy were flagged, and the flagged copies were split into short-5.8S
copies, at least 3~bp shorter than the culture's main type, and same-length copies. Where the medoid fell inside
the short-5.8S group, the flags came out inverted, and the classes were swapped (Table~\ref{tab:edits}).

\subsection*{Methods S3. Searches and candidate names}
The VT search used the MaarjAM gDAT snapshot of July 2020 \cite{opik2010maarjam}, the SH search UNITE release
10.0 of 19 February 2025 \cite{nilsson2019unite,unite2025release}, and the LSU search the Delavaux V18 database of
May 2025 \cite{delavaux2021lsu,delavaux2022lsu,delavaux2024lsu} (repository commit \texttt{f4a0014}). Searches used
BLAST+ \cite{camacho2009blast} with the settings in Table~\ref{tab:arms}. Many V18 records carry terminal N
padding, so coverage did not count reference Ns; without that correction, 95 of the 460 records would fail the
coverage test even against themselves. Hits to any accession of the culture reference were dropped. The name of a
culture in a system is the most common name of its main-type copies (ties went to the higher median identity). A
copy's names depend only on its fragment, the database and the dropped accessions, not on culture names. The
comparison of the names (the crosswalk) was run before the last two \spn{Septoglomus} edits
(Table~\ref{tab:edits}), so its report on cultures was replayed on the final labels with the stored names of each
copy (\path{crosswalk_replay.py}), after every stored name had been checked against evidence captured on the final
reference: in all three systems and for all \NCopiesRetained{} copies, the stored identity equaled that of the
highest-scoring hit passing the threshold, and the best name was present at that identity.

Each copy received a set of candidate names instead of a single best hit. The anchor was the copy's best hit by
bit score, and the set held every name within $\delta$ identity points of the anchor that also passed the
identity threshold. Names that split the main-type copies of one culture at $\delta=0$ were merged when the
closest copies across the split were within 1\% in the SSU flank; this limit keeps the two lineages of a mixed
culture from being merged. $\delta$ was set to the smallest margin at which the main-type copies of at least 99\%
of cultures share a name; for VTs it was also at least the 95th percentile of the SSU differences within cultures.
SHs and LSU names carry no such floor, because their variation within genomes is wide and uneven (95th
percentile: ITS 8.6\%, LROR--FLR2 3.9\%), and the merged names carry that variation instead
(Table~\ref{tab:sets}).

\subsection*{Methods S4. LSU placement}
The V18 references were realigned with MAFFT \cite{katoh2013mafft}, and branch lengths and GTR+$\Gamma_4$
parameters were fitted on the published topology with RAxML-NG \cite{kozlov2019raxmlng}. Queries were added with
\texttt{--addfragments --keeplength} and placed with EPA-ng \cite{barbera2019epang}. The compatible edges of a
query carry at least 95\% of its likelihood weight. Edges inside a maximal clade bearing a single V18 species label
were collapsed into one name (the LSU species clade). Where a species label is not monophyletic on the backbone,
its separate components stayed separate names, and edges that carry several species labels stayed individual
names.

\subsection*{Methods S5. Combining the names and the decision steps}
A name was linked to the species and genera of the training cultures whose candidate-name sets hold it. In each
system a copy is \emph{matched} (its name is carried) when its names belong to training cultures,
\emph{unmatched} (not carried) when it has a name but no training culture holds it, and \emph{silent} when it has
no name. Genera were intersected over the matched systems, and species over the matched systems whose names are
linked to named species. The repository's codes for the decision steps are: likely artifact, A1; divergent copy,
D1 (short 5.8S) and D2 (paralog name), with D3 for a same-length 5.8S that is only flagged; near a VT threshold,
B1; known species or species group, K1 (all matched systems agree and neither the VT nor the LSU name is
unmatched); new species in a known genus, N1 (VT unmatched), N2 (LSU unmatched), N3 (no species survives while a
genus does) and N6 (the ITS distance rule); discordant, X1 (the matched systems share no genus); and new lineage,
N4 (no system matched) and N5 (as N4, with the best VT also below the near-threshold band). The comparators of D1
are the main-type copies of the nearest training culture; the support of D2 is the number of other training
cultures that reach the name only through short-5.8S copies. Divergent copies are named by the VT alone. A copy is
scored against what the reference knows: main-type copies of species held by another training culture should
receive a known species or species group that contains their species; main-type copies of withheld species should
not receive a known species (otherwise a false-known call); and divergent copies should be recognized as
divergent or, for same-length copies, at least not be called new.

\subsection*{Methods S6. Withholding tests and the choice of percentile}
Every quantity derived from cultures was fitted on the training cultures of each round only: merged names,
$\delta$, the links from names to species, the comparators of D1, the support of D2, the pools of nearest copies
and the limit of the ITS distance rule. The test with withheld cultures had 148 rounds, each removing one physical
culture label together with all lineages sharing it; the test with withheld species had 50 rounds, one per named
species with all its cultures removed plus one per culture not named to species. In these tests MaarjAM, UNITE and
V18 stayed fixed. In the LSU-removal test, V18 was realigned without the target's records, the model refitted and
all copies placed again (Table~\ref{tab:m4folds}). The second version of the protocol added the ITS distance rule
(N6) and changed nothing else; \spn{Glomus chinense} and \spn{Glomus rugosae} informed that rule and are reported
as development rounds. The test species (the `first-outcome' rounds in the repository) are all other species: the
\MfourFolds{} whose V18 records were removed, and \spn{\MfourAbsentTarget}, which V18 does not hold by label,
reported apart. The same protocol had \GenusFolds{} genus rounds, in which every culture of a genus and the
genus's V18 records were withheld together.

To test the choice of percentile, the ITS distance rule and the complexes were set together at the 95th and
97.5th percentiles (Table~\ref{tab:percentile}): the rule was re-applied to the stored results of the decision
steps, with the limit of each round refitted on its training cultures (\path{percentile_sensitivity.py}).
Percentiles follow Python's exclusive quantile method throughout, as in the complex rule
(\path{species_groups.py}).

\subsection*{Methods S7. The independent test}
The overlap of LongGloDB with the Stefani et al.\ cultures was established with \path{longglodb_overlap.py}. The
protocol and its two amendments (\path{PROTOCOL_LGDB.md}) fixed the outcomes, conditions and analyses, and the
predictions of the complex rule were locked by hash before any test sequence was searched, placed or scored. The
frozen code of the second protocol version was used unchanged. All \NCultures{} reference cultures were used for
training and every test culture was excluded, so no test sequence entered merges, margins, name links,
comparators or the limit of the ITS distance rule. Test sequences were cut by the same fragment step as the
deposited copies, and their VT and SH evidence was computed with the same filtering against the same releases. In
condition~A the test fragments were placed on the intact V18; in condition~B each of the \LgFolds{} absent
species, all of which V18 holds by label, was removed from V18 in its own round, as in the LSU-removal test. The
secondary analysis (amendment~2) measured VT coverage over the part of each MaarjAM reference that the query
spans.

\subsection*{Methods S8. Trees, artifact and structure checks, and distances}
The protocol (\path{benchmarks/copy_checks/PROTOCOL_COPY_CHECKS.md}) was locked on 11 October 2026 and the
structure check (\path{ADDENDUM1_FUNCTION.md}) on 12 October 2026, each before its results were computed.

\emph{Trees.} The copies of each order (Archaeosporales 30, Paraglomerales 28, Diversisporales 245 and Glomerales
600) were analyzed separately, because the ITS cannot be aligned across orders and every culture and species lies
within one order. MAFFT v7.505 L-INS-i (\texttt{--localpair --maxiterate 1000}), without trimming; the linked
alignment joins the three alignments with one partition per region. IQ-TREE 2.3.6 with ModelFinder
(\texttt{-m MFP}; for the linked alignment, one model per partition with edge-linked proportional branch lengths),
1,000 ultrafast bootstraps (\texttt{-B 1000}), 1,000 SH-aLRT replicates (\texttt{--alrt 1000}), near-zero branches
collapsed (\texttt{-czb}) and seed 20261011. Each tip is one distinct sequence and stands for every copy that
carries it. A group is tested on a tree when it has at least two copies. It is \emph{unresolved} when no branch
separates it and none contradicts it (it sits in a polytomy, or shares an identical sequence with a copy of
another group); a branch that only joins copies with the same sequence has no support value and never counts as
strong. For a group that is not monophyletic, its \emph{escapees} are its copies outside the largest clade made
only of its copies. All 1,436 calls of monophyly were recomputed independently with Bio.Phylo, with the same
result.

\emph{Artifact check.} Each copy was aligned to the nearest main-type copy of its own culture (distance over the
whole copy; never itself) by global alignment with free end gaps (Biopython \texttt{PairwiseAligner}; match 2,
mismatch $-3$, gap opening $-5$ and extension $-2$, the BLASTN defaults). Gaps that touch either end of the
alignment are trimming differences and were ignored. Each internal deletion was assigned to the region of the
comparator (SSU flank, ITS1, 5.8S, ITS2, LSU flank) that holds most of its bases. Two copies are of the same type
when their full-ITS distance is at most 1.0\%, and the two lineages of a mixed label count as one isolate.
Sensitivity analyses fixed in advance: same type at 0.5\% and 2.0\%, and long deletions read as at least 10~nt in
every region.

\emph{Structure check.} Infernal 1.1.5 \texttt{cmalign}, with truncated alignments allowed. The groups were the
short-5.8S copies, the same-length copies, the main-type copies with a long deletion in the SSU flank, 5.8S or LSU
flank (\emph{flagged main}), and all other main-type copies with a main-type culture-mate (the baseline, which set
the 99th percentiles). The paired comparison set each divergent copy against the mean of the main-type copies of
its culture (two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test). The 5.8S deletions of the short copies were located on the
RF00002 consensus. The ITS2 was folded with ViennaRNA 2.7.2 \texttt{RNAfold} (default parameters, 37\,$^\circ$C):
minimum free energy per nucleotide, and the base-pair distance between the structure of a copy and that of its
comparator.

\emph{Distances.} For pairs of cultures, each culture was represented by its medoid main-type copy, the copy with
the smallest summed full-ITS distance to the other main-type copies of its culture. The within-species spread of
the NS31--AM1 fragment (\ThrVTSpread{}) was computed as for the three markers of the complex rule. The 28
Paraglomerales copies lack the NS31--AM1 fragment and were left out of that region.

\emph{Longer regions.} The Kr\"uger fragment was cut from each copy from the SSUmAf1 site \cite{kruger2009dna}
(found with at most one mismatch in all 903 copies) to the end of the copy at LSUmBr, so the last stretch of the
SSUmAf--LSUmAr amplicon is missing; the whole copy runs from AML1 to LSUmBr. Distances, classes and spreads are as
above. For a cut-off $t$, a main-type copy is split from its culture if it lies more than $t$ from every other copy
of its culture, and joined to another species if a copy of another species of its genus lies within $t$; the
partners of the four complexes are counted both ways (Figure~\ref{fig:thrlong}). Script:
\path{stage3/copy_long_fragments.py}.

\emph{What the short type does.} Short copies were counted by their 5.8S sequence: the short-5.8S class and every
copy whose 5.8S is identical to one of it, which adds PX214776.1. All retained copies of the named species were
clustered by single linkage within each order at 98.5\% and 97\% identity on the full ITS, the Kr\"uger fragment
and the whole copy, once with every copy and once with main-type copies only (Table~\ref{tab:clusters}). On the
Glomerales tree of each marker, rooted on a \spn{Funneliformis} tip, we recorded the clades made only of short
copies, their support, the taxa they join and the main-type taxa of their sister group. Script:
\path{stage3/copy_paralog_effects.py}.

\emph{A motif for the short type.} The motif YTYGGRGARTATG covers the short-type hairpin of every short copy of
\spn{Rhizophagus} and \spn{[Rhizoglomus]}. It was written from these copies and tested on the same copies, so it
shows that such a flag is possible, not its error rate. We searched it in the 5.8S and in the whole copy of all
\MotifCopies{} retained copies, and located the ITS2 primers fITS7 \cite{ihrmark2012new} and ITS3
\cite{white1990amplification} in the 5.8S, allowing up to two mismatches (ITS3 matched every \spn{Rhizophagus}
copy with one or two). Script: \path{stage3/short58_motif.py}; results in
\path{benchmarks/copy_checks/short58_motif}. As an independent test we searched the motif in all \UniteSeqs{}
sequences of the UNITE+INSD set of 19 February 2025 \cite{unite2025release}, which holds none of the culture
copies. A hit counted as in the 5.8S when fITS7 (up to two mismatches) started 30--45~nt before it, as in the
culture copies (37~nt), and a sequence spanned the site when fITS7 was found with at least 60~nt after its start.
SHs are the SH codes of the UNITE records. Script: \path{stage3/short58_motif_db.py}; results in
\path{benchmarks/copy_checks/short58_motif/unite}. These analyses are exploratory and fix no rule.
